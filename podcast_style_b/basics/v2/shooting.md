===== SCRIPT START =====

COACH: You've got the puck out by the boards in the other team's end. The net is right there, and you can see the goalie clearly.

PLAYER: So I shoot. Right?

COACH: Maybe. That's what this episode is about. This is The Basics, topic six. Shooting, and going to the net. The Basics is for newer players and younger players. It covers the core things to get right first. I'm the coach, and with me is a newer player, asking the questions you might ask. There are six ideas, and we'll take them one at a time.

COACH: First idea. Shoot from the middle. Stay with that moment. You're out wide by the boards, and a teammate is standing in the slot. The slot is the ice right in front of the other team's net, between the two faceoff circles. It runs from near the net out to the tops of those circles. What would you do?

PLAYER: Shoot anyway? I've got the puck.

COACH: Pass it to them. Try to take your shots from the slot. And if two strides toward the middle would put you in the slot, take those two strides first.

PLAYER: Is the slot really that much better?

COACH: Shots from the slot go in several times as often as shots from the outside. The outside means everywhere further out than the slot. Out by the boards, and up near the blue line. Not a little more often. Several times. So one pass, or two strides toward the middle, is worth more than any trick with your hands.

PLAYER: So what about a shot from way out?

COACH: A shot from far out, at a set goalie who can see it, with nobody in front, is one of the easiest saves in hockey. A set goalie is one who is still, facing you, and ready for your shot. Moving to the middle is the biggest thing you can do to score more.

COACH: Second idea. Shoot quickly, before the goalie is set. The puck comes to you in the slot. You stop, settle it, look up, plant your feet, and fire your hardest shot.

PLAYER: And the goalie saves it?

COACH: Usually. A goalie doesn't stop the puck by reacting to it. They watch your body, your hands and your stick, and get into position before the puck is on its way. If your shot arrives after they are set, it is an easy save, even if it is hard. If it arrives before they are set, it is a real chance, even if it isn't very hard.

PLAYER: So when I shoot matters more than how hard.

COACH: Yes. So shoot from where you already are. Don't stop to settle the puck first. And don't stop your feet. Shoot while you are still skating. Every little pause gives the goalie a warning. Settling the puck, looking up and planting your feet all take time. And stopping your feet is the loudest warning of all.

PLAYER: How do I actually take a shot like that?

COACH: Start with the basic one, the wrist shot. Start the puck behind your back foot. That's the foot further from the net. Sweep it forward along the ice, and don't slap at it. Keep the puck on your blade the whole way. Your blade is the flat part at the bottom of your stick. Move your weight from your back foot to your front foot as you sweep. Then follow through.

PLAYER: Where does the power come from?

COACH: Your legs and body, not just your arms.

PLAYER: And how do I keep it low, or get it up?

COACH: With your follow-through. Finish low and the puck stays low. Finish high and the puck goes high. Where your follow-through finishes is how you aim the height.

COACH: Third idea. Get the puck through to the net. The puck comes to you, and a defender is standing right between you and the net. Wanting to shoot is good. Take a good chance straight away, without needing to be talked into it. But don't shoot just because you have the puck.

PLAYER: What's wrong with shooting whenever I can?

COACH: Think about a shot that hits a defender's shin pad. You lose the puck. The goalie never had to do anything. And the other team often races the other way with it. That shot is worse than no shot at all.

PLAYER: How do I know if it'll get through?

COACH: Look for a lane. A lane is a clear path from your stick to the net, with no legs or sticks in the way. If you can't see the net, there is no lane.

PLAYER: And if there isn't one?

COACH: Step sideways a stride or two. The defender is set up for where you were a moment ago, so moving makes them move too. Or fake the shot, so they drop into the lane, then step round them. Or pass the puck instead, and wait for a better chance.

PLAYER: When a defender's right on me, I sometimes just fling it at the net, or pass it anywhere.

COACH: That's panic. A shot into a shin pad, or a pass you didn't look at, just gives the puck away, and usually you had more time than it felt. So look before the puck reaches you. Then you already know whether there's a lane, and which teammate is your second choice.

PLAYER: What about shooting from way out, near the blue line?

COACH: Then the job is to get the puck through, not to shoot it as hard as you can. Keep it low, at knee height or below.

PLAYER: Why keep it low from out there?

COACH: From out there, the shot is mostly for your teammates at the net. A tip is when a stick touches the puck on its way to the net and changes its direction. A rebound is when the puck bounces back out off the goalie. A low shot can be tipped or turned into a rebound. A high one is no use for a tip.

COACH: So before you shoot, ask yourself one question. What happens if this doesn't go in? If the answer is a rebound for a teammate, or a tip, then shoot. If the answer is that it hits a shin pad and they break away, don't.

COACH: Let's stop and check the first three, because they decide most goals. Can you give them back to me?

PLAYER: Shoot from the middle, or pass to a teammate who is there. Shoot quickly, from where I am, without stopping my feet. And find a lane first, so the puck gets through.

COACH: Spot on.

COACH: Fourth idea. Make the goalie move, and aim where the goalie isn't. Think about the goalie for a moment. They're set, square to you, and they can see the puck.

PLAYER: Then they'll save almost everything.

COACH: Right. So make them move first. Pass the puck across the middle of the ice before the shot. Or carry it from behind the net and out the other side. Then shoot as soon as the puck arrives.

PLAYER: What does a pass across the middle do?

COACH: It drags the goalie sideways. While they are still sliding across, they are not set. The far side of the net, down low, is the space they are moving away from.

PLAYER: So that's where I aim.

COACH: Yes. Aim for where the goalie isn't. Just after a pass across the middle, aim far side and low. The gap between the goalie's pads is called the five-hole. It opens while the goalie is moving, but it is closed when they are down and set.

PLAYER: What if my teammates are standing at the net?

COACH: Then shoot low and hard at the goalie's pads. That shot can go in, get tipped in, or bounce out as a rebound. That is three good chances, not one.

PLAYER: I've heard you should always aim for the corners.

COACH: Some coaches teach aiming for the corners instead. Both ways are taught by good coaches, so ask yours which they want.

COACH: Fifth idea. At the net, screen the goalie and get your stick on the puck. A teammate is about to shoot from out near the blue line, and you're near the net. Where do you stand?

PLAYER: Out of the way, so I don't block it?

COACH: Take away the goalie's eyes, not their room. Stand between the shooter and the goalie, where the goalie is trying to look, so they can't see the shot coming. That's called a screen. A goalie who can't see the puck has to guess. A screen turns an easy save into a guess.

PLAYER: How close do I get?

COACH: Stand just outside the crease. The crease is the goalie's area, the blue paint in front of the net. The red line around its edge counts as part of it, so keep your skates off that line too. Never touch the goalie. And never stand in the goalie's way back to their net.

PLAYER: What if a defender's pushing me?

COACH: A screen is a place you stand, not a push. Hold your spot with your position, not your weight. Leave the goalie room to move. If you get pushed into the blue paint, get your feet out before you play the puck.

PLAYER: What do I do with my stick?

COACH: Keep it on the ice, in the path of the shot. That is where a tip happens. Hold the stick loosely, and let the angle of your blade change the puck's direction. Don't swing at it.

PLAYER: Can a little tip really beat a hard shot?

COACH: Think about what the goalie has done. They've got ready for a shot going one way. A tip changes its direction right in front of them, too late for them to follow. Changing the puck's direction late beats making the shot harder.

PLAYER: How high can my stick go?

COACH: Keep it low when you tip, between your knees and your chest, and below everyone's shoulders. Bring it back down after the tip as well. You are in a crowd, and a high stick can hit someone in the face. A puck tipped with your stick up high may not count, either.

COACH: Now the most important safety part of this episode. Standing at the net. Defenders will try to move you. You are standing still, next to a steel goal frame, watching the shooter. So keep your head up and your chin off your chest, and never duck. If you meet a goal post, meet it with an arm or a leg, never your head.

PLAYER: Is there one reason for all that?

COACH: One. You want to see what is coming, and keep your head and neck out of it. Hitting a post head first can hurt your neck, even at walking speed.

COACH: Sixth idea. Follow the shot to the net. You shoot, and then you stand still to see whether it went in.

PLAYER: I do that all the time.

COACH: Don't stop and watch. When you shoot, or a teammate shoots, skate to the net with your stick on the ice.

PLAYER: What's the point, if the goalie has already saved it?

COACH: A goalie who has just made a save is getting back into position, not set. The rebound goes to whoever gets there. So if a rebound lands on your stick, shoot it straight away. Don't stop it and pick a corner. If the goalie is down, lift it over the pad.

PLAYER: And if the goalie's lying right there in front of me?

COACH: Play the puck, never the goalie. Pull up before you reach the goalie. And if a goalie has turned away from you, never go into their back. Stop, and take the space in front of the net instead.

PLAYER: Why so careful with the goalie?

COACH: A goalie who is down, or turned away, can't protect themselves. Running into a goalie can hurt them, it can be called a penalty, and it can cost your team the goal.

COACH: One more safety point, for practice. In the warm-up, keep your shots off your goalie's head, and shoot from a distance, not from right in close. And never shoot at the other team's goalie.

PLAYER: It's only the warm-up, though.

COACH: That's the problem. No whistle comes in time to protect them, and a mask can hide how hurt they are.

COACH: Let me put all six together.

COACH: Shoot from the middle. If a teammate is in the slot and you are not, pass.

COACH: Shoot quickly, from where you are, without stopping your feet.

COACH: Find a lane first. Ask what happens if it doesn't go in.

COACH: Make the goalie move, then aim where the goalie isn't. With teammates at the net, shoot low and hard at the pads.

COACH: At the net, stand just outside the blue paint and off its red line, never touch the goalie, and keep your stick on the ice and below everyone's shoulders. Head up, chin off your chest, never duck.

COACH: And follow every shot to the net. Shoot the rebound at once, and play the puck, never the goalie.

PLAYER: What should I ask my own coach?

COACH: Two questions. Do you want us aiming for the corners, or where the goalie isn't? And where do you want me when a teammate shoots? Their answers tell you how your team wants you to play.

COACH: Once these six ideas feel natural, you are ready for more. The longer Playing principles episode on shooting goes deeper into each idea. The conversation episode talks it all through. Start there next.
