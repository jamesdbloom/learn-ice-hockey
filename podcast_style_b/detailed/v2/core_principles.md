COACH: Then ask again every time you change team, league or age group. An adult moving between leagues is more exposed to this than a beginner, not less. And here, "whatever your coach wants" isn't the answer. A coach cannot make a body check legal in a non-checking game.

COACH: Twelfth idea, and it's short. Holding your spot. In most leagues you're allowed to hold the ice you're standing on. You don't have to move to let an opponent through. Except in the crease. If the goalie needs the ice, give it at once.

PLAYER: So I can take away a lane without hitting anyone.

COACH: That's exactly what it's for. And it's done with your feet and your position. Keep your stick down and your free hand off them. No push, no lean, and never your stick across anyone.

PLAYER: Is that true everywhere?

COACH: There's a catch in adult recreational leagues affiliated to CARHA, a body that runs adult recreational hockey. CARHA's rules let a defender stand their ground. The same rules also penalise deliberately bodying or shoving an opponent, standing in front of them to make contact, or not avoiding contact with them. That can be a minor, or a major that throws you out of the game with at least a one-game suspension. The rules never say how the two fit together. So find out whether your league is affiliated to CARHA before you rely on holding your ground.

COACH: Let me pull it all together.

COACH: You almost never have the puck, so play the rest of the shift. Defending, stay goal side, with your stick in the lane, and don't join the chase unless you were sent. When the puck goes to the net, go with it, unless you're the safety. Stop just outside the crease, off its line, stick on the ice, head up. Take the ice in front of the net, never the goalie. And never pass the puck, or skate it, across the front of your own goal.

COACH: Decide how you meet the boards on the way in. Go in on an angle. The boards position. Skates parallel, forearm and hip to the boards, never the point of your shoulder, head up, chin off your chest, never your back to anyone coming at you, never duck. Keep it up even going slowly. If you're already facing the wall with no time to turn, stick and gloves up and spread wide on the glass, head off it.

COACH: When you pressure, or chase, or race for a loose puck, it's the puck, not the player. Whether you may hit at all depends on your league and your age group. If you can see their numbers, pull up.

COACH: Look before the puck arrives, so you never have to panic pass. Give the carrier two places to put it, at different angles. Play simple in your own end and creative in theirs. And settle two things before you play. What system does your team play? And is your game a checking game?

PLAYER: What should I ask my own coach?

COACH: Three things. First, what system do we play in our own end, and what does the far winger do when the puck is in our corner? Second, on a shot, who stays back as the safety, and which way do we take the puck out of our end? Third, which competition am I registered in, is it a checking one, and if it's adult recreational hockey, is the league affiliated to CARHA? Their answers tell you how your team wants you to play, and now you know why each answer makes sense.
