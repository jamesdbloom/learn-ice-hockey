# SG57: the late-hit limit on "body on the man", and prohibition-first goalmouth wording (23 October 2026)

## What changed

- **playing_without_the_puck.** "Stick on the puck, body on the man, in that order" never limited the body to a player who still has the puck. Heard as a sequence, it teaches the late hit, or "finishing" the check. The limit was added in every layer that carries the claim:
  - **Key focus :20:** "and only on the player who has the puck".
  - **Overview :40:** "and only on the player with the puck". An interim wording, "only while they have it", could be heard as "while the other team has the puck", because the paragraph before it uses those words for team possession; the reviewer caught it.
  - **Facts :196:** "body on the man while they still have it".
  - **Body :211:** "Only while they still have the puck: once it has gone, let up, because a check after a pass or shot can be a late hit", linked to body_contact_and_battles' late-hit section. "Can be" fits every book (see below), where an interim "is" overstated the NHL and IIHF.
  - **Q2 drill:** "on whom may you use it".
- **goaltender** (:1361 In short, :1369 facts, :1383, Common Mistakes :1635, KT12 :1732) and **defender** (:13 Key focus, :312 facts, :319, Common Mistakes :906, KT3 :985).
  - The active "take it / send it toward the corner" could be heard by a Hockey Canada non-checking or CARHA player as an instruction to push.
  - It now leads with the prohibition, matching body_contact :1388: "never let it cross your goalmouth — the corner side is the safe one".
  - Every no-push limit and every "coaching caution, not a written rule" disclosure stays.
  - KT12's "Never from behind:" fragment now reads "Never hit them from behind:", and its ⚠️ is kept.
