## What changed

**special_teams (W253): the British fight opener.**
- **The defect.** "In Britain a fight late in a game can end yours" implied that lateness is the trigger. That understated the cost for three groups:
  - British women's hockey: under In-House Rule 101, any major ejects.
  - The instigator: IIHF v1.1 46.3 is an automatic game misconduct at any point.
  - British competition outside NIHL, the SNL and BUIHA Varsity: In-House Section 6 scopes the softening to those leagues, and elsewhere the IIHF stands. That is v1.1 Table 6, or 2026/27 46.1, a major plus an automatic game misconduct.
- **Opener, now:** "Do not fight, and never be the one who starts it. ⚠️ **In Britain, plan on a fight ending your game.** Only NIHL, the SNL, BUIHA Varsity and the Elite League make a plain fight during play a major without an automatic ejection — never in women's hockey, and in NIHL not in the last five minutes or overtime." The England-and-Wales one-game ban follows.
- **Same claim, every layer.** :1093 gains the IIHF-elsewhere sentence. :1095 opens on the Elite League's own tiers. CM :463 is split into two sentences, with all four limbs. Facts :436 names all three triggers and cites "IIHF v1.1 46.3".
- **The coordinator's brief was wrong, and the author refuted it.** The brief said the Elite League ejects only the instigator. The EIHL Casebook (46.1 Note, 46.2) also gives a game misconduct for a second major and for fighting after the original altercation has started.

**forechecking_systems (W254): the arrival cue.**
- **The defect.** "Feet under you for the last two or three strides" could be heard as striding into contact. The thresholds differ by book: Hockey Canada 7.4 "two or more strides"; CARHA 52(a) Note "more than two steps or strides"; USA Hockey 607 Note "more than two".
- **New wording, at five sites** (Key focus :20, In short :242, facts :246, body :256, CM :1098), using the wording reviewed on offensive_zone_play: "stop striding two or three strides out, keep your feet under you, and arrive on the puck or the ice beside the carrier".
- **Women's limit.** Each cue now shares its spoken chunk with the women's-hockey limit, "never step or glide into her / an opponent" (IIHF 101.1, PWHL 52.1). KT2 gains "a step or glide".
- **Excessive distance.** The :260 warning now says Hockey Canada, USA Hockey and the IIHF all add an excessive-distance test. That matters now that the cue tells players to stop striding.

**shooting (W255): goalie hits from behind.**
- **Head counterweight.** The Hockey Canada "off the head" carve-out gained its head counterweight, "on it, a match if they cannot protect themselves" (7.6(c), 7.7(c)), at facts :286 and :506, body :534 and CM :945.
- **Facts :506 (300/300)** now uses the reviewer's measured line: "A goalie's back: no book has a bare minor (…). Driven helpless into the net: mandatory match…".
- **CM :945** gains USA Hockey 608(c)'s mandatory match with all three conditions (reckless, excessive force, vulnerable or defenceless). W236 had missed this list.
- **The crease-charging sentence (:528)** was split from 97 words into four sentences. No book or tier was lost, and all four sentences are still in chunk 035.
