# Wave 38 (15 October 2026): how to play is the strong focus

## What ran

The owner gave this direction on 15 October, quoted exactly: *"lets make sure the content is more focused on how to play and that is the strong focus, rules are fine to include but the focus should be on what to do not on the rules, readers of the content what to know how to play"* (sic). It is Priority 1 in the plan, and this wave was aimed at it alone.

The wave ran in four steps:
- **Content agents.** 15 agents worked on disjoint file groups across the play pages: positions, systems, technique and hockey-IQ. They:
  - turned Key Takeaways into situation-and-action kernels;
  - made each Common Mistakes bullet open with its instruction;
  - made Key focus and Overview about play;
  - opened body sections on the play;
  - added the missing situations as craft, giving the common approach, a realistic alternative, and "ask your bench".

  Rule ladders moved into the body and facts only after a per-limb layer test.
- **Reviews.** Three safety reviewers and two tactics reviewers, all read-only, split by dimension.
- **Fix round.** Nine agents.
- **Last read.** A final reader checked the fix round, and its three minors were repaired.

The 15 content agents were launched as one workflow run. The owner had not asked for workflows, and the user was told.

**Owner ruling this wave.** On 15 October the owner replaced "never your back to the boards / wall" with "never turn your back to the checker", across the corpus. A source check found that no safety source uses the old phrase. Read literally, it means the opposite: USA Hockey's Heads Up guide lists "their back" as a right way to hit the boards. **This wave did not touch that phrase**, and the agents were told not to. The replacement is its own wave (plan row [W38 back-to-the-checker]).

## What this method could not have found

- **Tactics the wave deleted.** The reviewers read added lines. A situation-and-action point lost along with the tariffs (for example in playing_without_the_puck, where Key Takeaways went from about 10.1k to 5.9k characters) would show only in a per-unit comparison of old and new.
- **Tactics the whole corpus teaches the same way but eccentrically.** Cross-document consistency cannot see these. The posture inversion was one such case.
- **How new craft performs when executed badly at speed.** No rulebook search tests this.
- **The final one-clause repairs** in the last-read row. They have no fresh reader.
