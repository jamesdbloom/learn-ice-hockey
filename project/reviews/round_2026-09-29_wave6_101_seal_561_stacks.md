## What the reads found

- **The IIHF women's 101.1 seal, settled.** The census's contradiction (body_contact "women may seal" against forechecking/OZP "the seal is gone") is resolved on the rule text:
  - A positional seal is allowed **only on ice you reached first**: *"hold their ground … any time that they have established their position"*, and the opponent *"is obliged to skate around"* a stationary player.
  - Any *"step or glide into"* her is at least a minor.
  - Using the boards to push, pin or eliminate her (lean her out of the play) is a minor, or a major and an automatic game misconduct.
  - The pursuit sentence (*"push and lean into each other"*) does not protect a late arriver. The corpus reading is harsher than the book only for two players racing side by side for a loose puck away from the boards, which is accepted.
  - The coordinator eye-checked the opener on the rendered page p.154 of `iihf_rules_v1.1.pdf`.
- **Both directions were live, often in one file.**
  - *Harsher:* "the seal itself is gone", "not at all in IIHF women's hockey", and the boards limb applied to every book.
  - *Permissive:* "let the boards do the rest", a false "one published exception", a late-arriving pinch told to "hold the ice", and "for its own sake" as an intent condition 101.1 lacks.
  - Several **summary layers were built on the old answer**: body_contact Key focus and KT1, DTR Overview, forechecking :580 and host prose :488.
- **IIHF 56.1 stick lifts: two repair agents ruled in opposite directions, and a rules-verifier settled it.**
  - 56.1 names *"receiving a pass"* only in its definition. Its "Stick:" limb routes stick impeding to hooking, and 55.1 exempts stick-to-stick contact.
  - USAH Casebook SoP Sit. 3 approves a press *"Just prior to the puck being received"*.
  - **Adopted:** lift or press as the puck arrives, low, and let go. The IIHF pass reading appears at most once per document, as a disclosed reading.
