**Source under consideration.** Johnston, Mike; Walter, Ryan. *Hockey Plays and
Strategies*, 2nd edition (Human Kinetics). Kindle ASIN `B07GX64NNL`, owned.
Print-replica/fixed-layout — every page is an image, so there is no text layer
and nothing can be extracted mechanically. It has to be read.

**What kind of source it is.** A coaching manual by two NHL coaches. Under this
corpus's evidence rules that makes it strong evidence for **conventions** — what
coaches teach, what systems are named, which options a system offers — and **no
evidence at all for rules**. Nothing from this book may become a `Rule:` line in
a facts block or a rule claim in a body. Rules still come from primary rulebook
text on disk, per non-negotiable 2.

## Part 1 — How to use it without copying it

The distinction that matters: **the systems are facts about the sport; the
book's expression of them is the authors' property.** A 1-2-2 forecheck, a
left-wing lock, a wedge+1 penalty kill — these are facts. Anyone may describe
them. What is protected is Johnston & Walter's wording, their diagrams, their
selection and arrangement, and their original drill designs.

So the book gets used in five ways, none of which copies anything:

**1. Corroboration.** Where the book agrees with a claim the corpus already
makes, confidence rises and the claim gets a second independent source. Record
the agreement; do not import the phrasing. This is worth the most on claims the
corpus currently supports from a single source.

**2. Contradiction — the highest-value output.** Where the book disagrees with
the corpus, that is a defect signal. It does not automatically mean the corpus
is wrong (a coaching book states one school's preference), but every
disagreement is a thing to resolve and, where genuine, to say plainly that two
respected sources differ. The corpus already does this well elsewhere.

**3. Gap-finding.** A list of *topics* the book covers and the corpus does not
is not protectable expression — it is an index. Where a gap is real, the section
gets written from primary sources in the corpus's own voice and structure, not
from the book's.

**4. Cardinal-rule auditing.** This is where the book is most useful and least
replaceable. Non-negotiable 7 says never present a coaching choice as a law of
hockey. Two NHL coaches setting out several legitimate options for the same
situation is exactly the evidence needed to catch places where the corpus has
quietly hardened one option into "the" way — or, equally, where the corpus
hedges something that really is near-universal.

**5. Ordinary citation.** Where a specific claim genuinely rests on this book,
cite it as the corpus cites its other books — author, edition, page — and quote
only if a short quotation is actually necessary. That is normal sourcing, not
copying.

**What must not happen:** reproducing or closely re-drawing their diagrams;
sentence-by-sentence paraphrase; lifting their drills; or adopting their chapter
structure as the corpus's structure. If a section of the corpus starts to track
the book's order and emphasis, that is the warning sign.

## Part 2 — The validation checklist

Read only the chapters bearing on these. Everything below is a claim the corpus
already makes, so each one is a straight agree / disagree / not-covered.

### Forechecking — `content/systems/forechecking_systems.md`
- Systems are named by counting outward from the goal line in three groups of five.
- The corpus's honest position that **no published count exists** of what teams actually run, so neither the 2-1-2 nor the 1-2-2 can be called "the most common" as data. Does the book assert a most-common? If so it is a coaching opinion and must be labelled as one.
- 2-1-2: F1 angles, F2 attacks the first passing *option* not the puck, F3 high in the middle. **Is F3 weak-side or strong-side by default?** The corpus calls this a genuine coaching split and tells the reader to ask — check whether the book takes a side.
- 1-2-2: the dog / fox / hawk roles, and the rotation where F3 becomes the new fox on a puck across.
- 1-3-1: the line of three is F2, F3 and **one defenceman**, with the D in the middle as read-maker.
- 1-4 and the passive 1-3-1 are really neutral-zone structures, not forechecks.
- Left-wing lock: Czechoslovak 1970s origin, adapted for the narrow rink by Barry Smith at Detroit.

### Neutral zone — `content/systems/neutral_zone_systems.md`
- A neutral-zone 1-2-2 is **not automatically "the trap"** — the trap is one particular setting of it.
- Regroups: D-to-D and low regroup, and the claim that they are under-used at amateur level.
- Stand up at the line vs back off — the corpus declines to declare a winner. Does the book?

### Breakouts — `content/systems/breakouts.md`
- The named options: Up, Wheel, Reverse, D-to-D, Rim, Over/cross-ice, Quick-up, Stretch, off the glass.
- The five roles, and the centre's swing.
- **The winger's four options and the order they are taken in — the corpus explicitly calls the order a coaching choice.** Prime candidate for cardinal-rule auditing.
- Breaking out against each named forecheck.

### Defensive zone — `content/systems/defensive_zone_coverage.md`
- Low zone collapse, man-on-man, hybrid, box+1, collapsing box / strong-side overload.
- The weak-side winger's collapse as the moment that decides the system.
- What each system concedes — the corpus's comparison table.

### Offensive zone — `content/systems/offensive_zone_play.md`
- Home plate / the scoring area; shot quality over quantity.
- The royal road / slot-line pass.
- Cycling mechanics, and the failure mode of cycling for its own sake.
- Low-to-high, half-wall options, behind the net, F3's job on a shot, the D's pinch.

### Zone entries — `content/systems/zone_entries.md`
- Carry-in vs dump-in and what the corpus says the evidence shows.
- The five dump types, and the delay / curl-back.
- Drop passes and why they fail so often.

### Special teams — `content/systems/special_teams.md`
- PP formations: 1-3-1, umbrella, overload, spread/box, 2-3.
- PP plays: seam pass, off-wing one-timer, royal road, backdoor, walk-down.
- PK formations: box, diamond, wedge+1 (triangle-and-one), and aggressive vs passive.
- PK entry-killing and the four pressure schemes.

### Faceoffs — `content/systems/faceoffs.md`
- Technique and alignments only. **All the rules content in this document —
  violations, ejections, the icing exception, closing the hand — stays sourced
  to the rulebooks and is out of scope for this book.**

## Part 3 — Out of scope for this source

Explicitly declared, so silence on them is not mistaken for a pass:

- Everything in `content/foundation/` — rules primer, UK rules.
- All `Rule:` facts lines anywhere in the corpus.
- Safety, contact legality, concussion and medical claims (`safety-reviewer`'s
  territory, and a tactics book is not authority for any of it).
- Equipment, conditioning, officiating.
- Goaltending technique beyond the goalie's role within a team system.

**To unblock:** System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording → enable

plan above exists to be picked up cold afterwards.

the page, repeat — targeted at the chapters in Part 2 rather than the whole book.
