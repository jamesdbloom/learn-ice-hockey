## The repairs were reviewed, and three of them were wrong

`rules-verifier` was run on the three rule claims rewritten above, against primary text. It
found that **two of my repairs introduced errors of their own**, and that a third was

**A wrong rule number, where the line it replaced had none.** Finding 6 rewrote `center.md`'s
faceoff `Rule:` fact as *"Closing your hand on the puck **or smothering it** … is a minor …
(NHL Rule 67.2)"*. **67.2 is a hand rule end to end** — catch it, place a hand over it, pick it
up (`sources/nhl_rules.txt:6961-6973`). It contains no body clause. Smothering with the body is
**63.2(v)**, *"On a player other than the goalkeeper who deliberately falls on or gathers the
puck into his body"* (`:6700`). The old line cited nothing and was merely vague; **the new one
named a rule a reader could look up and not find their conduct in.** That is a regression, and
it was introduced by the repair. Now cites 67.2 and 63.2(v) separately.

**A carve-out both books state and my wording described backwards.** The same fact said *"a
skater … who does it **inside his own crease**"*. Both books make the **puck's** location
decisive, not the player's. NHL 63.6 (`:6797-6811`): *"The significant factor … is the location
of the puck at the time it was held, grabbed or gathered into the body. If the puck is in the
crease, penalty shot. If the puck is outside the crease and gathered into the body of a player
(other than the goalkeeper) who is inside the crease, minor penalty."* My phrasing described
precisely the case the rulebook carves out. Fixed in `center.md`'s fact, and in
`defensive_zone_coverage.md`'s bullet, fact and body, which had the same ambiguity.

**And a mis-attribution that reinstated, in the summary layer, the exact trap the body exists
to prevent.** Finding 7 replaced `defender.md`'s author-facing instruction with the dimensions
— and attributed *"twenty-eight feet apart"* to **NHL Rule 1.8**. **1.8 states no span.** It
gives per-post offsets only: *"eight feet (8') from each goal post" … "eleven feet (11') from
the goal post"* (`:826-833`). The only place "twenty-eight feet (28') apart" is printed is
**27.8** — the rule the same fact tells the reader not to use. `defender.md:133` says this in
terms, and my summary-layer line contradicted its own body. Now attributes the offsets to 1.8
and the span to 27.8.

That last one also had a wrong emphasis worth recording: *"27.8 still prints the narrower
figure"* is true **only at the goal line** (six feet per post against 1.8's eight). At the end
boards the two rules agree exactly, and 27.8's 28 ft is the **current** figure and the corpus's
only source for it. Heard aloud, the singular invited a listener to discount the one number in
27.8 that is live.

**One flagged claim adjudicated, and it changed the corpus.** `zone_entries.md:174` stated NHL
83.4 truly but absolutely. The verifier ruled the exception belongs in the attacking-side
block, on textual grounds rather than taste: 83.4's closing sentence is **the only one in the

## And the last text written was read by nobody, which the gate caught

The review sequence was `content-reviewer` → repairs → `rules-verifier` → repairs →
`safety-reviewer` → repairs → `facts-reviewer` → repairs. **Nothing ran after the last stage**,
and the last stage produced new rule claims, a rewritten body paragraph, a fact **split** in two

words. The gate re-derived those passages itself and found them sound, and then found the one
they had all missed:

⚠️ **USA Hockey 618(a) was cited for an act it does not reach — the same defect this record
calls "the round's most important paragraph", recurring one book over.** 618(a) is **hand-only**:
*"picks the puck up off the ice with their hand or holds the puck."* The clause covering the body
is **614(b)**: *"falls on the puck, gathers the puck into their body or uses the hands to hold
the puck while in the goal crease … A goal shall be awarded … if the goalkeeper has been
removed."* Two sites cited 618(a) in sentences whose subject was smothering with the body. Both

USA Hockey form of the determinant this round propagated from NHL 63.6.

conduct in.** The first was 67.2 for a body act; this was 618(a) for a body act. The failure is
not carelessness about numbers — it is reaching for the rule that governs the *neighbouring*
act, in a corpus where hand and body are separate clauses in all four books.
