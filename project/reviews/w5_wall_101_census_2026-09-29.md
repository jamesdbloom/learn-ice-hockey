# W5 census: sealing, pinning or riding an opponent on the wall, checked against IIHF women's Rule 101.1 (29 September 2026)

Read-only census. **No `content/` file was edited.** This file is the only thing written.

## Premise, re-verified

`sources/iihf_rules_2026-27.txt:7591-7604` (and `iihf_rules.txt:7491-7504`, `iihf_rules_v1.0.txt:7499-7512`,
`iihf_rules_v1.1.txt:~7504`) all carry the same text. After flattening, the wording is identical in the 2026-27 and older editions:

> "If two (2) or more Players are competing for "possession of the puck", they are not allowed to use the boards
> to make contact with an opponent to eliminate her from the play, push her into the boards, or pin her along the boards."

The penalty clause, *"(I) Minor penalty (2') (II) Major penalty (5') and automatic game misconduct penalty"*, is printed
**before** the boards paragraph and reaches it through *"an illegal hit on an opponent described in this rule"*. So the
premise's tariff holds, **but it holds by the rule's structure.** The boards sentence does not state it again.

Two limbs of the same rule matter for this census:
- *"reasonably allowed to push and lean into each other provided that 'possession of the puck' remains the sole object"*. This is a permission.
- The boards sentence is the exception to it. **The rule never uses the word "seal".** Whether a positional seal counts as "using the boards to make contact … to eliminate her from the play" is a **reading**. This census does not settle it.

**Not verified here:** that 101.1 governs "every level" of British women's hockey. That is the corpus's own claim, and its owner is `uk_rules.md`. I did not re-check it.

## Method

1. Split every `content/*.md` into units: facts lines, list items and paragraphs. Each unit is tagged with its layer from its `##` section.
2. Flattened each unit (lower-case, markup stripped, whitespace collapsed).
3. Kept units that contain a wall term (`boards|wall|glass|dasher|corner`) **and** an act verb within 120 chars of it. The act verbs searched: seal, pin, ride, rub out / rub-out, finish (a) check, angle X into/to/along, squeeze, press/push/drive/force/steer X, hold/tie up, lean on/into, eliminate. The ungated pass returned 741 units; the proximity gate left 281. I read all 281.
4. A second pass without the wall gate covered hold/trap/squeeze/close off/separate…from the puck/rub out/finish him. That pass caught `body_contact_and_battles.md:265`, which the first pass missed because its unit names no wall.
5. Rendered all 39 documents with `md_to_speech.py --out <own scratch>/ssml`: 39 documents, 3,586 chunks. For each candidate, I located its chunk by a word sequence and tested whether that chunk contains `women` or "one hundred and one".

**Verdict key.** **PERMISSIVE** means a women's-IIHF reader who acts on the unit alone, or on its chunk alone, commits a 101.1 illegal hit. **READING** means permissive only if a positional seal counts as "using the boards". **CARRIES** means the exception is in the unit or its chunk. **DESCRIPTIVE** means the unit describes an opponent doing the act to the reader and gives no instruction to do it.

## C. The finding under the table: the corpus disagrees with itself on whether a seal survives 101.1

- **C1. Seal survives:** body_contact_and_battles.md:1121: "in IIHF women's hockey … the first player in **seals** and goes for the puck; she does not pin the player … **Seal, and take the puck.**" The same document's :218/:209 scope 101.1 to "the pin" in §7/§8 only, which leaves §4's seal (:415/:428) and §1's :61 standing as universal.
- **C2. Seal is gone:** forechecking_systems.md:543 ("in a book that still allows it, which is not the IIHF book in women's hockey"), :580 ("rule 101.1's boards limb takes the seal itself away"), :590 ("Never: seal a winger against the boards in IIHF women's hockey"), :904 ("In IIHF women's hockey the boards seal goes as well"); offensive_zone_play.md:874 and :1169.

Both readings are defensible from the rule text, because 101.1 bars *using the boards to make contact … to eliminate* and does not say "seal". **They cannot both ship.** A women's reader of body_contact is told to seal, and a women's reader of forechecking is told never to. This census does not rule on which reading is right. **It needs a `rules-verifier` ruling on the act, then a claim-wide brief across both documents in every layer.** C2 is the conservative reading, which this project ships where a book is silent or ambiguous. On C2, rows 1-4 and 6 are PERMISSIVE. On C1, rows 7-14, 16 and 19 are still PERMISSIVE by their own documents' readings, because those documents adopt C2.

## What this method could not have found

- **An act described with no wall word near the verb.** Row 4 was found only by a second, un-gated pass. The proximity gate (120 chars) can miss an instruction whose "wall" is in the previous sentence or the section heading, such as "end the angle with a seal" inside a section about angling to the boards. More such units may exist that I did not reach.
- **The chunk test is lexical.** It looks for `women` or "one hundred and one" in the chunk. A chunk can mention women's hockey for another reason, such as an age line or the USA Hockey girls' classification, and score as CARRIES without stating the boards limb. I read CARRIES rows only where the unit itself quoted 101.1. Rows 17 and 20 were judged by chunk and were not read in full chunk text.
- **Diagram captions.** They were not swept. `check_caption_hosts.py` would show whether a caption says "seal" or "ride" into a host document.
- **The site's rendered order.** Chunk placement was measured. Whether a reader on the page meets the carve-out before acting was not.
- **Whether 101.1 really governs every level of British women's hockey** (the uk_rules.md claim) and whether In-House Rule 101's ejection statement applies. Neither was re-verified in this session.
- **Other books' own boards-contact limbs.** PWHL 52.1 (women's pro) permits angling "using their body in order to separate her opponent from the puck". I did not test whether the corpus's PWHL units are right about the boards. That is a separate women's reader population, and this census did not cover it.
