## Why
SG139 took nine carried OPEN_ITEMS rows on five pages, one authoring agent per page, each told to refute its row first. Two of the rows turned out to be permissive (an act told as cheaper than it is), not harsh as they had been filed:
- **puck_handling:** the cage line gave the NHL no route to a match on injury (75.2(ii)).
- **forechecking:** :811/:812 gave USA Hockey only the "forceful at the boards" trigger. Neither line carried head first or reckless.

Review then found two more permissive units of the same kind:
- **special_teams KT10:** "Every other book can eject" told a USA Hockey reader a charge on a goalie that isn't reckless cannot eject. 607(a)/(c) allow a major plus game misconduct.
- **winger :530:** the same "can in every other book" shape. A coordinator census found no other sites.

**SG140** is the claim behind the forechecking finding, briefed across the corpus. USA Hockey Casebook 608 Situation 1 says a major plus game misconduct, or a match, "must be called" for three cases:
- a forceful check on a player standing along the boards;
- a player "thrust head first into the boards or goal frame";
- a reckless check, with or without board contact.

608(b) independently makes head first and reckless a mandatory major plus game misconduct. A facts line or summary unit that names the boards trigger alone, voiced alone, tells a USA Hockey player that a head-first drive or a reckless hit stays at 608(a)'s minor plus misconduct. A census of "forceful" units found this on dzc, faceoffs, ozp, zone_entries, dtr and pwtp, beyond forechecking.
