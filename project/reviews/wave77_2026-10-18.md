# Wave 77 (18 October 2026): the owner's head-first ruling, applied corpus-wide

## The ruling

On 18 October the owner chose "Harsher of the two". It refines the 15 October ruling:

- A Hockey Canada hit from behind that **reaches the head** is priced as head contact under 7.6 (minor and female) or 7.7 (Junior and Senior), as before.
- A hit that **drives a player head first into the boards or goal frame** also carries the 7.5 price:
  - at least a minor plus a game misconduct (7.5(a), (e));
  - a major plus a game misconduct if he is hurt (7.5(b));
  - a mandatory match if he cannot protect or defend himself (7.5(c)).
- Where the hit does both, **the harsher price stands**.

The basis is hc.txt :6139-6141, where 7.5's example (ii) reads "driven head-first into the boards". The rules-verifier memo (Q1–Q5) set out why this matters:

- 7.6(a)'s floor is a minor or double minor, and 7.6(d) allows no misconduct. That is cheaper than 7.5(a)/(e).
- 7.6(c)/7.7(c)'s match is worded "hits a player in the head", so it may not reach a head-first impact.

## How the ruling was carried

Every repairer kept the 15 October ruling for a pure head hit: 7.6/7.7 only, with no "head contact too". Where a head-first drive also reaches the head, they wrote "the harsher of the two prices stands". They never let 7.6's quoted "supersedes any other rules" be the last word, or the "because", of a head-first discussion.
