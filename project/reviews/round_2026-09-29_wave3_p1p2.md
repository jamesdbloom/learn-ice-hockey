## Method lessons, for the next wave

1. **Read the whole answer a quotation comes from before briefing it.** The coordinator's half of UKCG FAQ 6
   became a Major.
2. **A sketch is a claim.** "Safe" was checked against no owner and was weaker than the owner's frame.
3. **A true negative stops a search.** 610(a) was a correct citation of the wrong half. Read the book's whole
   rule on the ACT (632 in full).
4. **A moved qualifier changes jobs.** "The same conduct" became "frustration aimed at an opponent", which covers
   a punch.
5. **Split a paragraph by rendering it.** The briefed split point stranded a permissive quotation at a chunk end.
6. **Coordinator scans with bounded quantifiers.** One `grep -oE` ran for 120 seconds on a bounded quantifier
   (ugrep). Use Python.

## What this method could not have found

- **Sibling documents** that repeat a repaired claim. Wave 4 (the CARHA 30(a) census) and the plan rows cover
  the ones found.
- **Whether a tactical instruction is the best coaching.**
- **Audio by ear.** Chunk placement was measured; nobody listened.
- **A layer built on an old premise with no lexical trace.** The D13 Major was found only by reading HEAD's
  takeaway against the new one.
