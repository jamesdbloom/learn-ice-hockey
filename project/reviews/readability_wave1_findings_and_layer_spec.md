# Part 1 — the plan's structural instructions are unsafe as written

Three of them. Each was verified against code, not inferred.

## ⚠️ 1. `<details>` and `[!NOTE]` are read aloud as garbage

The plan says *"do not rely on CSS `details`, visual collapse, or a table of contents to define the audio
order. … A collapsed visual section is still spoken unless the renderer's behaviour is deliberately
changed and tested."*

**Both halves are false, and the second is false in both directions.** `scripts/md_to_speech.py`'s
`parse_blocks` has no HTML branch, so a `<details>` element falls through to the paragraph arm. Measured
on a probe document, the emitted speech is:

```
<p>less than details greater than less than summary greater than Go deeper: the offside rule …</p>
```

`SYMBOLS` maps `<` to `" less than "` and `/` to `" or "`. The *content* is spoken — and so are the tags.
`find_residue` does not flag it because every character survives in `ALLOWED_RESIDUE`.

A GitHub-style callout is no better: `> [!WARNING]` becomes an ordinary blockquote and the marker leaks
into the audio as the literal word `!WARNING`, **with no `"Important. "` prefix**. The only construct that
earns spoken emphasis is the literal `⚠` glyph, which the corpus already uses.

**And the converse the plan misses entirely: collapse-on-site plus silence-in-audio already exists, with
no renderer change.** `remark-corpus.mjs` wraps the Sources trailer in `<details class="sources">` while
`md_to_speech.py` drops it — along with everything after it, `## Notes on verification`, and any paragraph
whose text *starts* with one of eight verification markers. `md_to_speech.py:471-492` records that this
divergence is deliberate and that the two files share vocabulary while doing opposite things.

**So the honest sentence is not "collapsed is still spoken". It is: visual collapse and audio inclusion
are independently controlled, they already disagree for three layers, and nothing checks that they agree.**

**RULE: no `<details>`, no `[!NOTE]`-style callout, in `content/`. Progressive disclosure is by heading
order. Emphasis is the `⚠` glyph.**

## ⚠️ 2. Only `##` is structural. A layer built on `###` is invisible to the chunker

```python
# md_to_speech.py:3126-3128
if block.level == 2:
    current = Chunk(plain(to_speech(block.lines[0], Counter())).strip())
    chunks.append(current)
```

`###` and below produce a spoken line with a 700 ms pause and nothing else. **A four-layer structure built
on `###` would be a visual structure with no audio existence.**

**RULE: every layer the plan names gets a `##`, or it is not a layer.**

## ⚠️ 3. The guarantee is the SENTENCE, not the unit

The plan requires *"every required condition remains in the same unit as its instruction."* **"Unit" is
ambiguous and the natural reading is wrong.** There are two: the `<p>` and the chunk. A `<p>` can be split
across two chunks — `_split_paragraph` (`md_to_speech.py:3368-3495`) cuts an oversized paragraph at
sentence boundaries. `check_chunk_tails.py:164-165` says so itself: *"Fold the limit into the SAME
SENTENCE as the permission; the chunker splits only at sentence ends."*

Worse, **chunk membership is not a stable property of a paragraph.** Packing is cumulative, so an edit
anywhere re-packs every chunk below it. The plan's *"save the extracted spoken units and verify"* is a
sound method with an artefact that goes stale on the next edit to the same file.

**RULE: a required safety, rule-set, age, league or medical condition goes in the SAME SENTENCE as its
instruction. Same paragraph is not enough. This is stricter than the plan and it is the only boundary the
renderer will not cut.**

## And a fourth: frontmatter would be read aloud

There is no frontmatter anywhere in `content/` — `site/src/content.config.ts:7-9` records that the corpus
predates the site and the collection schema is deliberately empty. Probed: **YAML frontmatter renders as
the first spoken paragraph of the document, ahead of the title.** `docs-meta.json` cannot hold hand-written
metadata either — it is gitignored and fully regenerated by `extract-meta.mjs` on every build.

**RULE: the metadata layer is a NEW TRACKED JSON manifest beside `structure.json`. Not frontmatter, not
`docs-meta.json`.**

# Part 2 — the principle inventory, and what it actually found

Two agents covered all 38 documents between them. **The plan's Phase 1 premise — "the small set of
cross-scenario principles that guide the rest of the corpus … scattered across position and systems
pages" — is half true, and the half that is true is not the half the plan expects.**

`grep -rn -i "core principle" content/` returns **zero hits corpus-wide**. Nothing in `content/` names
itself a principle. There is no principles layer to extract; there is one to *derive*, from evidence, and
have reviewed as claims.

## The three highest-frequency recurring items are not principles

Measured, not estimated:

| Item | Spread | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| The boards-posture safety override | **13 files, 66 occurrences** of `skates parallel to the`; `chin off your chest` in 14 | a **safety constraint** |
| "Find out which system / book / league applies to you" | **21 of 26** files in one agent's scope; all 12 in the other's | a **meta-instruction about local variation** |
| F1/F2/F3 by order of arrival | **14 files** | a **vocabulary convention** |

**A principles index built by frequency would be two-thirds not-principles.** And roughly half of what
reads like a principle is labelled `Convention:` by its own owner, often in the ` ```facts ` layer where a
listener hears it alone — promoting those is the cardinal-rule failure with extra steps.

## The two agents agreed where they overlapped, and one answered the other's open question

Agent A (foundation, getting-started, positions, reading-diagrams) ended with a declared gap: three of its
documents credit `systems/forechecking_systems.md` with owning the F1/F2/F3 definition, and it could not
open that file. **Agent B found it at `forechecking_systems.md:88-103` and quoted it** — *"F1, F2 and F3
are defined by order of arrival at the puck. Nothing else."* The owner holds it. That is the handover
`CLAUDE.md` calls the highest-value work available, and it closed with no extra dispatch.

Where the two scopes overlapped they agreed on classification every time: "somebody stays above the puck"
is `system-convention` in both (both found the same three bench-called suspensions); the boards posture is
`safety-constraint` in both; scanning is `evidence-backed-observation` in both.

## Four findings that must be settled before anything enters an index

**F1 — the anticipation principle is stated two ways, at the same prominence, unsourced and ownerless.**
`scanning_and_anticipation.md:452`: *"Anticipation is acting on information. Guessing is acting on hope."*
with a one-question test at `:456`. `risk_management.md:509`: *"Anticipation is acting on information.
Gambling is acting on hope."* with a **three**-question test at `:535-539`. Both at Key Takeaway
prominence (KT9 and KT7). Neither cites the other. No `Convention:` label, no source, no owner row in the
style guide. This is the corpus's cleanest genuinely transferable decision rule **and** the exact shape of
the style guide's *"Claims that 'everyone knows' are the least audited"*. **It needs an owner and either a
source or a label before it enters an index, not after.**

**F2 — an owner-bare / borrower-hedged asymmetry on a contact instruction.** *"Be inside, force them
outside"* is stated **bare** in `rink_map.md` (§5 and a Key Takeaway) and **hedged** in `defender.md` KT2
and `switching_positions.md`, where it carries a four-book division-scope stack. **The shorter, flatter
statement is the one a principles page would naturally source from, and the two documents carrying the
qualification are the ones a consistency sweep would "correct".** This is the style guide's *"When the
honest document is the outlier"* shape exactly. Handed to `rules-verifier` — running now.

**F3 — two conflicting vocabularies in adjacent sections of one document.**
`puck_support_and_spacing.md` §4 *"Support angles: behind, level and ahead"* and §5 *"Above, level and
below the puck"*. §4's "level" means same distance from your own net, across the ice; §5's means neither
goal-side nor puck-side. **§5 explicitly warns its vocabulary trips people up and does not mention the
section immediately above it.** Not a release-one pilot file; recorded as an open row.

**F4 — four candidate principles rest on untested negative-existence disclosures.** The style guide
records that when this class was finally attacked, **every disclosure tested was false**, and several had
their falsifier in the Sources list of the file that disclaimed them. If a disclosure is false, an index
would restate the falsehood somewhere more prominent than it currently sits. Handed to `source-verifier`
— running now.

## The rejected-candidate log did real work

Both agents kept one, as the plan requires. Two entries worth recording because they are the failure mode
the plan is trying to prevent:

- *"The house default is a 2-1-2 forecheck and a low zone collapse"* — appears in 14 documents and
  **must never enter an index.** `forechecking_systems.md:813` says why in terms: *"The house default
  throughout is a 2-1-2 because the position documents are written around it"*, and `breakouts.md:604`:
  *"The default is a documentation convention, not a claim about what most teams play."*
- *"A change of speed beats top speed"* — five sites, four of which label it coaching craft with two named

  and explicitly not to sweep it, because the style guide records that a previous prescription to sweep
  this exact claim would have "corrected" four documents that were already right.

# Part 3 — the pilots, measured against the budget

| Budget | `getting_started.md` | `rules_primer.md` |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 600 primary-path readable words | **16,424** (27×) | **83,727** (140×) |
| ≤ 5 min audio | 58.3 min shipped (12×) | 67.9 min shipped (14×) |
| ≤ 5 primary-path headings | **55** | **65** |
| ≤ 3 clicks from homepage | **1** ✅ | **1** ✅ |
| ≤ 200 words to first actionable instruction | **266** (or 195, or 502 — see below) | **95** ✅ |

**Click depth is not the binding constraint. Word count and heading count are.**

## ⚠️ The first-instruction measurement is undefined, and the budget passes or fails on the definition

For `getting_started.md` there are three defensible answers and the plan supplies no rule:

| Words in | Line | Why it is or is not the answer |
|---|---|---|
| 195 | *"Public skating or a learn-to-skate class first, then a one-off taster…"* | the first concrete next step — but stated as description, not imperative. **If this counts, the budget is met.** |
| **266** | *"**Start a level below where your pride puts you.**"* | the first imperative addressed to the reader |
| 502 | *"**Before you turn up to any session, ask what gear is required, what standard it is, and whether there is contact.**"* | the first **safety-bearing** actionable instruction |

**Decision recorded here: the measurement is the first SAFETY-BEARING actionable instruction where the
document has one, and the first imperative otherwise.** Reason: the budget exists so a reader reaches
something that protects them early, and 195 measures a sentence that protects nobody. By that definition
`getting_started.md` is at **502 words, 2.5× budget**, and that is the number the rewrite must move.

## ⚠️ `rules_primer.md` meets the first-instruction budget on the letter and defeats it on the intent

The first instruction is the Overview's opening clause at 95 words — *"Find out which rulebook your league
plays under before your first game, because the books disagree, and one of the disagreements can cost you
the rest of the match."* **It is the opening of a 4,467-character single-paragraph line.** The reader gets
the instruction, then 700+ words of four-book comparison before the next one.

## ⚠️ The 600-word primary path is not achievable for `rules_primer.md`, and the reason is structural

**52% of its 478 body lines name at least one rulebook; 40% name two or more and set them against each
other.** Rule-set scope is not a category of statement inside this document — **it is the document.** Plan
working rule 4 therefore applies to essentially every sentence of the primary path, not to a list of
exceptions.

**The plan's own §"Candidate treatments" asks for a "short first-game rules path plus optional rule-set
reference". The measurement's conclusion is that such a path is a NEW LAYER, not a compression of this
one.** The plan permits an exception to the 600-word default with a written reason; **this is that
written reason.** See the layer spec below for what release one ships instead.

## Two structural facts that shape the rewrite

- **`rules_primer.md` §4 Penalties is 26,804 readable words — 32% of the whole primary path** — and has
  **no diagram at all**, while all nine of the document's diagrams cluster in §2/§3/§5/§7/§8.
- **`## Common Mistakes` at 10,611 words is not a summary layer, it is a second body.** Three of its
  H3s individually exceed the entire average section of `getting_started.md`.

## ⚠️ A table costs the listener 5,616 words, and its own "unverified" label is in the layer nobody hears

`rules_primer.md:848` is 6 columns × 23 rows with a 2,448-character cell — over on all three of
`check_tables.py`'s limits, so the speech renderer replaces it with *"A detailed table appears here in the
written version."* §10 totals 8,163 readable words; **5,616 of them are in that table.** Every
NHL/IIHF/Home-Countries/USA-Hockey/rec divergence the section exists to state never reaches audio.

**`check_pointers.py` cannot see this**, because no spoken sentence names the table. And the qualifier on
its sixth column — *"Unverified: the 'typical rec / beer league' column in section 10 remains a
generalisation"* — sits in the **Sources trailer, which is not voiced at all.** A dropped table, with its
honest limit in a silent layer. **This is an open row, not something release one fixes by deleting a
table.**

Separately confirmed: **`rules_primer.md:753` sits at exactly zero rows of headroom** — adding one row
drops it. `CLAUDE.md` names this file as the one found by running `--near` rather than by reading a plan,
and the measurement confirms it independently.

## ⚠️ `## Check yourself` conflicts with the plan's own acceptance criteria

Both pilots carry one (8 questions each), and **both are voiced** — a listener is asked eight questions
with no answer layer. The preamble instructs the reader to *"answer each question before you go on — a
real attempt, not a guess."*

The plan requires that *"the primary pilot path is fully understandable without answering, clicking to
reveal an answer, or performing an activity. Optional exercises remain visibly and audibly optional."*

**These are reconcilable and the plan says how: the section stays, and its preamble is rewritten so it is
visibly and audibly optional.** That is a small edit inside each pilot and it is in release-one scope.
**Deleting the section is not** — it would be stripping a layer the plan explicitly preserves.

## One disclosure is deleted from the audio outright

`getting_started.md:240`, a ⚠️ Verification note about a source failing in two different ways in six
weeks. A paragraph is dropped when its text *starts* with a verification marker. It is the only such
paragraph in either pilot.

## And `check_disclosures.py` misreports the layer for this file

The tool classes `rules_primer.md` lines 1081, 1083, 1093 and 1095/1097 as `body`. **All are inside the
Sources trailer and none is spoken** — confirmed by grepping the rendered speech for five distinctive
strings from those lines: **zero matches.** `is_voiced` accepts a line on any seven-word run matching
anywhere in the speech, and the trailer lines are the longest in the corpus, so the collision is
near-certain. The effective split is **68 body / 6 unvoiced, not 73/1**. **A tool defect, coordinator-owned,
recorded as an open row — not fixed mid-round, because `CLAUDE.md` records what happened the last time a
shared tool changed underneath live agents.**

# Part 5 — the layer spec for release one

Everything above constrains this. It is the specification the pilots and the principles index build to,
and it is deliberately smaller than the plan's seven layers.

## The decision: ONE new `##` layer, and the other six map onto contracts that already exist

Plan working rule 6 says *"Preserve the facts layer, Common Mistakes, Key Takeaways, Sources, and audio
contracts."* Six of the seven named layers already have a home. Inventing parallel headings beside them
would duplicate the summary layer and give a listener two recaps.

| Plan layer | Release-one home |
|---|---|
| 1. Core principles | `content/foundation/core_principles.md`, a new document |
| 2. **Key focus** | ⭐ **NEW `## Key focus`**, immediately after the rule-set banner, before `## Overview` |
| 3. Simple summary | the existing `## Key Takeaways` — the style guide already defines it as *"5–10 items, each standalone and memorable"* |
| 4. Recognise it | a labelled paragraph inside `## Key focus`, plus existing in-body cues. **Not its own `##`** — the corpus already states cues in substance everywhere and a separate heading would strand them |
| 5. Understand it | the existing body |
| 6. If your team differs | existing rule-set and system qualifications, in place beside their claims. **They must NOT be gathered into one section** — that would separate a condition from its instruction, which Part 1 rule 3 forbids |
| 7. Go deeper | existing deep sections, with the boundary named in `## Key focus` rather than by moving text |

## The `## Key focus` contract

1. **`##`, not `###`.** Part 1, rule 2.
2. **Immediately after the `> **Rule set:**` banner, before `## Overview`.** It must be above the
   Sources trailer by a wide margin — anything below that trailer is silent.
3. **≤ 250 readable words.** It is the bounded primary path, and the document below it is the reference
   layer. **This is the written exception to the plan's 600-word whole-document default**, and Part 3
   gives the reason: for `rules_primer.md` a 600-word primary path is not a compression, it is a new
   document.
4. **Every required safety, rule-set, age, league or medical condition sits in the SAME SENTENCE as its
   instruction.** Part 1, rule 3. Not the next sentence, not the next paragraph, not a footnote, not the
   Sources trailer.
5. **No `<details>`, no `[!NOTE]`.** Part 1, rule 1. `⚠` is the emphasis mechanism.
6. **No new table.** `check_tables.py`'s limits are 3 columns, 14 rows, 200 characters per cell, and
   `rules_primer.md:753` is already at zero rows of headroom.
7. **It states no claim that is not already in the document below it**, re-derived from the primary source
   at the moment of writing. It is a promotion of existing verified material to the top, not new prose —
   **and where it re-words, the re-wording is new text and has not been reviewed.**
8. **It names where the reader goes next**, including the boundary to the reference layer.

## What release one does NOT do

Recorded so that a later round does not mistake omission for oversight:

- **No corpus-wide rewrite.** Two pilots and one new document.
- **No facts block is added to either pilot.** Both are among the 12 documents that have none, the style
  guide states the exclusion with its reason, and adding one would be a new spoken layer and would move
  `check_facts.py`'s in-scope count from 26.
- **No diagram is retired, added or redrawn.** Phase 3 is a later release.
- **No table is deleted**, including the dropped one at `rules_primer.md:848`. Its cost is recorded as an
  open row.
- **No `## Check yourself` section is removed.** Its preamble is made optional; the questions stay.
- **No audio duration target.** Restated as the unit contract in Part 1 rule 3, on the owner's decision,
  because episode length is nearly independent of document length (r = 0.301) and no short episode exists
  anywhere in the corpus to shorten toward.
- **`check_disclosures.py` is not fixed mid-round.** Tool changes happen between waves.

# What wave 1 could not have found

- **Whether any of it is true.** Five agents read the corpus and the code. **No rulebook was opened and no
  citation refetched in wave 1** — every `rule-dependent` classification above is a classification of what
  the corpus *says*, not of what the books say. Wave 2's `rules-verifier` and `source-verifier` are running
  against exactly that gap.
- **The rendered page.** No agent built the site or opened a browser. Every claim about the homepage,
  navigation and search above is from source. Whether 38 cards actually overwhelm a reader, what the
  38-item sidebar does at 375px, and the viewport half of the first-instruction budget are all unmeasured.
- **Diagram captions.** They are voiced, they live in `site/src/diagrams/*.mjs` rather than `content/`, and
  **neither inventory agent read one.** A principle stated — or stated *bare* — only in a caption is
  invisible to every grep in this record.
- **What the accepted candidates sound like voiced alone.** Several are stated as `Never:` facts values,
  which the style guide records as the corpus's most dangerous sentence form. The spoken extraction for
  both pilots is saved; **nobody has walked the 364 and 619 `<p>` units** to confirm each condition sits
  with its instruction.
- **Concept coverage, as opposed to phrase coverage.** Every frequency figure in Part 2 is a grep for a
  particular wording. A document that teaches a principle in different words counts as zero. The counts are
  **lower bounds on statement, and say nothing about application** — "5 of 12 state it" may sit on top of
  "12 of 12 assume it".
- **Whether these are the right principles.** Both inventories derived candidates by reading for recurrence
  and cross-scenario reach. A reader deriving them by asking *"what does a U10 player need before their
  first session"* would produce a different and probably shorter list — **and that reader is the one the
  plan's acceptance criteria are written for.**
