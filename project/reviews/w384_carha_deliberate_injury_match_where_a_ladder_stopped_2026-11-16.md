## Source
**The W383 read-only census of CARHA Rule 48(a).** It looked for the mandatory match for anyone who "deliberately injures or attempts to injure an opponent … in any manner". The census found that the corpus priced many CARHA fouls whose own rule has no match limb (hooking, holding, tripping, interference, charging, body contact) as topping out at a major.

**Carried by:**
- W381 (5) and W382 (3);
- W382's R2 finding that 48(a) reaches a hook.

**What W384 fixed, and what it did not.** It fixed only three shapes:
- **(W) a stated ceiling**, e.g. "up to a major";
- **(L) a closed ladder** that ends at a major;
- **(A) an asymmetric unit**, where another book's match is named beside CARHA's major.

A unit that prices CARHA silently, naming no top, was left alone on purpose. A deliberate injury is an act, not a technique, and adding 48(a) to every CARHA mention would be paranoia, not readability.

  - PWHL 42.4 and 50.4 matches exist but are unpriced at :472 and :496.
  - Other fouls whose Hockey Canada rule bars a match, where 7.1(c) would still apply, are unchecked.
- **game_management :1192** "by body" leaves out the equipment that 50(a) also covers. It says less than the rule, not a permission, and is cap-bound.
- **Hockey Canada's own conflict.** For a trip or hook meant to injure, 8.6(c) and 8.2(c) bar a match while 7.1(c) and Appendix D Note 1 give one. The corpus's "plan on one" is a reading of that conflict, not a quoted rule.
- **CARHA ladders worded without "major"** (e.g. "ends your game"), and the other 31 documents, were not swept for a ladder that stops at a major.
