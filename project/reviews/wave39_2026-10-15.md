# Wave 39 (15 October 2026): "never turn your back to the checker"

## What ran

The corpus told players "never your back to the boards / never your back to the wall" about 169 times across 28 documents. A tactics reviewer in wave 38 pointed out that, read literally, the phrase means the opposite of what it intends. A source-verifier then confirmed four things:

- **No safety source uses the phrase.** USA Hockey's Introduction To Body Contact says "facing the boards" for the dangerous case, and "skates parallel to the boards", "forearm and upper thigh (hip area)" and "Heads Up – Don't Duck" for the safe one.
- **USA Hockey's Heads Up guide counts hitting the boards with "their back" as a right answer** (`sources/huh.txt:551-564`).
- **The rulebooks frame the hazard as turning your back towards an opponent:** IIHF 43.1, NHL 43.1 and Hockey Canada 7.5.
- **The literal reading had already caused a real error.** switching_positions invented a disagreement with crossicehockey.com, which teaches "put their back to the boards to receive the pass". That means boards behind you, facing the ice, which is the same reception this guide teaches.

Earlier rounds (26, 31 and 71, plus an archived 3 September plan entry) had raised this and nobody had ruled.

**Owner ruling, 15 October:** replace it with **"never turn your back to the checker"**. The owning section (body_contact_and_battles) adds once: "that is, never turn to face the wall with them behind you".

**How the wave ran:**
- **Rewrite:** ten agents owned disjoint file sets, nine for content and one for diagram captions. They rewrote rather than swapped every site.
- **Review:** two safety reviewers read every change literally.
- **Fix round:** the findings were fixed.
- **Last read:** a final reader checked the fixed text.

## What changed

**The phrase.**
- The canonical spoken unit, where the full instruction belongs: "skates parallel to the boards, forearm and hip, head up and chin off your chest — never turn your back to the checker, and never duck."
- Self-contradicting sites were rewritten, not swapped:
  - breakouts "contact arriving behind you with your back to the boards" (physically incoherent) became "facing the wall with the checker behind you".
  - winger, center, defender and zone_entries had the fallback order inverted. They now say: side-on is the target; facing the wall square is the last resort, with stick and gloves on the glass; turning your back to the checker is never an option.
  - puck_handling taught "take the puck facing the wall or facing away from the checker". It now teaches side-on, hip and backside to them.
  - switching_positions' disagreement with the coaching source is withdrawn. Read correctly, the source teaches the same reception, and Breakouts adds a step off the wall.

**Scoping found by review.**
- Unscoped, "never turn your back to the checker" forbids the delay-and-curl move. That move deliberately turns away from a defender in open ice.
- Where a unit teaches the curl (zone_entries, offensive_zone_play, the `entry-delay-curl` caption), the ban is now scoped to "an oncoming checker near the wall". This is the owner section's own heading scope.
- On retrievals (faceoffs, DZC) the player necessarily skates back to the corner with the forechecker behind. The ban there reads "take the contact with your back to the checker" / "when the checker arrives, never have your back to him".

**Other fixes in the same files.**
- **body_contact :1322 and :1837:** Hockey Canada Interpretation 1 to 7.5(a) is scoped. A stationary cross-check from behind is cross-checking; one in motion or into the boards is checking from behind. The old text overstated it, and the new text is not permissive, because 9.2(b) still allows a major plus game misconduct with no injury.
- **zone_entries KT11/CM:** a first carve-out wording, briefed by the coordinator as a "loose puck", was permissive. An agent caught it against Casebook 607 Situation 5. The second wording left "privileged area" undefined in a unit voiced alone. Both units are now back to the unconditional rule, and the defined carve-out lives in the body at :755.
- **puck_handling:** the heading "Back into the checker" sat four lines from the new rule. It is now "Side-on into the checker", with the anchor and references updated. The spin-off move now starts from side-on.
