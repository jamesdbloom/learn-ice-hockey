## The census that finally escaped its own trap, and said so

Five previous passes censused the corpus for unsourced claims about what leagues do. **Every one
keyed on a QUANTIFIER** — `most`, `many`, `few`, `usually`, `commonly` — near a population noun. The
fifth ended:

> *"…sentences like 'rec leagues don't call this', 'your league won't have one', 'nobody enforces
> that below junior' are the same claim with the quantifier deleted, and match nothing I ran. That is
> the fifth time this trap has been named in this repository and **I did not escape it either — I
> widened the vocabulary, I did not change the method.**"*

The sixth pass keyed on the **population noun** and read every sentence that named one: **1,555
lines across all 37 documents, layer-tagged** (983 body · 186 facts · 138 Sources · 86 Key Takeaways
· 64 Common Mistakes · 52 Overview · 39 Check-yourself · 7 Notes-on-verification), plus all **112
diagram caption and describe fields**.

### The number the pass exists to produce

**18 sites the previous method could not have reached.**

- **11 carry no quantifier at all** — invisible to all five prior censuses regardless of how far
  their vocabulary was widened. *"visors are not universal in beer-league play"*, *"Bench discipline
  is worse in rec hockey than anywhere else"*, *"Rec-league rosters are filled by word of mouth"*,
  *"In rec and beer league, where nobody has practised rotations"*, *"so many British rinks have no
  trapezoid painted on them"* — asserted causally, in a document that elsewhere says no census of
  British rinks exists.
- **4 are in the diagram-caption layer**, which **no census of `content/` by any keying can reach.**
- **3 are inversions the noun found and a quantifier census would have MIS-RANKED** — a hedged block
  beside a superlative body, a `Rule:` label sharing a line with a prevalence claim, and an owner
  contradicting itself in three lines. ⚠️ **A quantifier census hits all three lines but has no
  reason to compare them.**

### ⚠️ And the more important half: ~240 sites read and CLEARED

*"The great majority are book-level statements naming their rulebook and rule number, scope
statements, or hedged practice observations… **Two-thirds of this corpus's league-noun sentences are
doing exactly what the style guide asks.**"*

Ten negative-existence disclosures were attacked directly and **all ten upheld**, including one —
`special_teams.md`'s power-play prevalence hedge — that is correctly hedged in **body, facts block
and caption**, the only claim in the census hedged in all three layers.

## The agent fetched real data, and then refused to over-claim it

The standing plan row said a governing-body registration figure *"would convert a dozen prevalence
claims from labelled residue into fact."*

It fetched the **IIHF Survey of Players** (November 2024) — `usahockey.com/membershipstatistics`
returns **404**; the survey is the document that actually exists and serves:

| | Total players | Indoor rinks (IIHF size / smaller) |
|---|---|---|
| United States | **566,450** | 2,092 / 138 |
| Canada | **587,680** | 2,860 / n/a |
| Great Britain | **12,236** | **45 / 8** |

⚠️ **And then refused the conclusion the plan row wanted:**

> *"This does not settle the claim, and must not be written as though it does. The survey is completed
> by the member national association, so those are USA Hockey's and Hockey Canada's own registration
> counts — **a numerator with no denominator.** It counts nobody playing outside those two
> registrations: CARHA-affiliated leagues, ASHL, municipal beer leagues, unaffiliated adult hockey."*

**What it gives is a citable figure to put where an unquantified "most" now sits — an improvement,
not a settlement.** ⚠️ **Writing it as settlement would be the compression defect the style guide
records.** The row was mine and the correction is right.

⚠️ **One disclosure narrowed rather than deleted.** *"No published census of British rink dimensions"*
is **upheld** — the survey classifies against "IIHF size", not the In-House Rules' 56 m × 26 m
threshold, and publishes no dimensions. But the corpus now has an adjacent published figure it does
not mention (**45 IIHF-size and 8 smaller British rinks**), and the honest move is to narrow the
disclosure to *"no census of British rink DIMENSIONS"* and cite the survey's size-class count.

## The largest single family: one claim, 17 copies, 15 documents, 10 formulations

*"Most North American rec and youth hockey runs on USA Hockey or Hockey Canada."* Previously counted
at eleven; it is **17 sites and none is sourced.**

⚠️ **It is load-bearing.** In most of the seventeen it is the sentence that tells a reader **which of
the four rulebooks applies to them** — for offside, closed hand on the puck, kicked pucks,
high-sticking, penalty shots, faceoff stick order.

> **The style guide's own rule applies: a claim generated once and stamped into fifteen documents is
> one unsourced claim with seventeen copies, not seventeen corroborations.**

⚠️ **And one strand is contradicted by the corpus's own evidence.** `rules_primer.md` says rec leagues
*"mostly adopt USA Hockey or Hockey Canada rules **with local amendments**"* — while its own §10,
twelve lines below, establishes that **CARHA is a standalone replacement adopted wholesale**,
differing on icing (65(a) automatic), body contact (49(a) makes failing to avert contact the
offence), slap shots (79(a), banned outright) and period structure (84(a), two twenty-minute
periods). **For CARHA-affiliated adult hockey the "with local amendments" framing is false, by the
document's own reading of the book.**

⚠️ **The repair must not remove the sentence's JOB.** Its function is to route a reader to their
book. The defect is asserting a distribution as measured fact. **Keep the routing; label the
distribution.**

## A disclosure that was true when written and is now false — and the successor story was wrong too

`equipment.md` said of the Canadian neck-protector certification: *"That announcement has not come
yet."* **It came on 2026-08-06**, and it does not say what the corpus expected.

BNQ's live certification page now carries, twice in rendered text, **"NOTICE - Closure of program
BNQ 9415-370 (July 31, 2027)"**, and the linked notice reads:

> *"following the recent publication of the international standard ISO 10256-5, the BNQ conducted a
> review of its Program and decided to discontinue it. Likewise, **the BNQ has decided not to
> implement a new certification program based on the new ISO 10256-5 standard**."*
> *"**No new applications for certification will be accepted.**"*

⚠️ **This is not a stale date. It contradicts the SHAPE of the corpus's story.** The file presents
CAN/BNQ 0102-565/2023 as the standard the programme moves *to*. **BNQ is leaving neck-protector
certification altogether**, directing companies *"to other accredited certification bodies."*

⚠️ **And the same file states that Hockey Canada Rule 3.6(c) makes a BNQ-certified throat protector
MANDATORY** for minor and female hockey, goaltenders included. **A Canadian reader is being told to
buy against a certification that stops being issued, by a body that has said it will not replace it.**

**The class is new here and worth naming.** Every stale-source finding this project has recorded so
far was a *link* that rotted or a *number* that moved. This is a **disclosure that was accurate,
sourced, dated and honest — and was overtaken by an event.** The corpus did nothing wrong; the world
moved. ⚠️ **Nothing in the process detects that.** `check_external_links.py` sees an HTTP 200; the
quotation still appears verbatim on the page; the honest-disclosure protections of non-negotiable 4
keep it in place. **A refetching pass is the only thing that finds it, and one had not been run for
this claim since it was written.**

## `pdftotext -layout` produces a FALSE ABSENCE on two-column prose

⚠️ **My brief told the agent to use `-layout` for anything tabular. That is right for tables and
wrong for two-column prose**, and the failure landed on a safeguarding claim.

The USA Hockey handbook is set in two columns. `-layout` interleaves them line by line, so a
whitespace-flattened grep for the SafeSport training scope returned **zero hits and looked exactly
like an absence.** **Slicing the left 48 characters of each line found it verbatim.**

**This is the extraction-trap family in a new costume**, and it is the one that matters most, because
the artefact is indistinguishable from a true negative and the corpus's negative-existence claims are
built on exactly this operation.
