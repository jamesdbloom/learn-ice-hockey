## Coordinator decision 1: Principle 1's scope is "on-ice tactical systems," not "any coaching choice"

Batch 1 flagged this as an unresolved ambiguity producing inconsistent matrix
results across waves. Reading Principle 1's own text in
`core_principles.md` (§1) settles it without needing a new rule: the
principle's **"What to notice"** test is explicit — *"You are probably
looking at a system rather than a law whenever the instruction is about
where a teammate goes rather than about what the referee will do."* Its
worked example (the weak-side winger's job in a low-zone collapse versus
man/hybrid coverage) and its **"A realistic alternative"** section (forecheck
structure, defensive-zone coverage) are both about on-ice positional
assignment specifically, not about coaching choices in general.

**Decision: Principle 1 applies where a document states a *positional or
structural* coaching choice — who covers what ice, who follows whom, which
system a team runs — and does not apply merely because a document contains
*some* coaching judgement call.** A document like `mental_game.md` or
`practice_and_development.md` making a non-positional coaching choice (a
periodization philosophy, a visualization technique) is not a Principle 1
instance by virtue of being a judgement call alone; it needs its own
justification or a different principle. This narrows batch 1's
`language_and_glossary.md` and `on_ice_communication.md` ratings (both
correctly re-derived under the narrow reading already, entry-by-entry, in
that batch's own report) and should be applied by any future wave using this
matrix, including a re-check of the first tranche's P1 column, which was
drafted before this scope question was raised.

**Not decided here, flagged for the next matrix wave to re-check under this
scope:** whether any of the first tranche's ten P1 "Applies" ratings would
change under the narrow reading. Spot-checking against the condensed matrix
table in the first draft, none of the ten look wrong under the narrow
reading (all ten hits are positional/structural: coverage assignment,
forecheck structure, faceoff alignment, special-teams shape) — but this is
an inference from the table, not a re-read, and is named here as a residual
rather than closed.

## Coordinator decision 2: a document may carry more than one framing principle where the matrix shows a genuine multi-principle fit

Raised by batch 1 for `mental_game.md` and `practice_and_development.md`
(both genuinely multi-principle) and by batch 2 for `on_ice_communication.md`
(a real two-way tie between Principle 4, which the document operationalises
via communication rather than a carrier's own scan, and Principle 7, which
appears near-verbatim in its own Key focus). **Decision: a single-frame
requirement is not a corpus-wide rule** — it was never stated as one in
`READABILITY_AND_DUAL_AUDIENCE.md`, which only requires that reference
material *may* carry none. Where two principles both genuinely fit a
document's own organising structure, the wave that writes the link should
pick the stronger of the two as the page-level frame and cross-link the
other at section level, rather than crowding both into one opening
paragraph or arbitrarily suppressing one. **This is a default, not a
resolution of `on_ice_communication.md`'s specific P4-vs-P7 tie** — batch 2
explicitly declined to choose between them, on the grounds that the choice
affects how the page's opening reads and belongs to whoever writes that
sentence, not to a brief. That choice remains open for the wave that
eventually touches this file.

## Verified: the cross-document rules-propagation risk batch 3 flagged does not exist

Batch 3's report warned that today's checking-from-behind rules-accuracy fix
(applied to `forechecking_systems.md` and `body_contact_and_battles.md`,
correcting a flattened "both books eject" claim to state USA Hockey's actual
non-ejecting default separately from Hockey Canada's ejecting one) might not
have propagated to four sibling systems documents that also discuss
checking from behind: `breakouts.md`, `offensive_zone_play.md`,
`zone_entries.md`, `game_management.md`.

**Checked directly, coordinator-level, against all four files:**

- `game_management.md` contains no checking-from-behind content at all.
- `breakouts.md` and `offensive_zone_play.md`'s only occurrences of the
  phrase are inside their Sources trailers, citing USA Hockey's own
  Declaration of Player Safety routing clause (which *names*
  checking-from-behind as one of several rules a dangerous check must be
  penalised under) — not a substantive claim about which tier applies, and
  not the flattened pattern.
- `zone_entries.md` states the scenario in full elsewhere in its body (the
  delayed-offside forecheck on a retreating, unaware defenceman) and already
  distinguishes USA Hockey's Casebook **aggravated** tier (major plus game
  misconduct, or a match penalty — an ejecting tier, correctly, because this
  specific picture matches Casebook Situation 1's "forcefully checks an
  opponent standing along the boards") from its own non-aggravated default,
  and states Hockey Canada's tier (7.5(a)/(b), also ejecting) separately and
  accurately. This is not the flattening defect — it is the same
  aggravated-versus-default distinction the fix itself introduced, already
  present and correctly scoped.

**Conclusion: no propagation gap exists in these four documents.** The risk
was a reasonable one to flag without having read the target files — batch 3
said so itself — and reading them settles it rather than leaving it open.

## Principle 4's goaltender gap: found by batch 2, fixed by the coordinator, under independent review

Batch 2's self-audit of `core_principles.md` found a Major defect: Principle
4 ("Look before the puck reaches you") is stated as an unqualified universal
claim in three places in the same document — the Key focus line, the
Principle 4 body section, and Key Takeaway 5 — and it inverts for a
goaltender tracking a live shot, whose whole job is the opposite: never look
away, track the puck all the way in. `core_principles.md` never mentions a
goaltender anywhere. This is the same shape as the already-known
`goaltender.md`/Principle 4 timing mismatch recorded in Workstream 1D, but
found independently, in the principles' own source document rather than in
a downstream position page, and found to run through all three layers
rather than one.

**Fixed, coordinator-authored, citing `goaltender.md`'s own already-verified
wording** ("track the puck all the way into the pad, the glove, the chest,"
a `Technique:` fact already on that page) rather than inventing new content.
All three layers — Key focus, the Principle 4 body's "The principle"
paragraph, and Key Takeaway 5 — now carry the qualifier. `check_links.py`
and `check_facts.py` both pass (5,621 facts, unchanged — no facts block was
touched).

**Independent `content-reviewer` pass: no Critical, no Major.** The
reviewer confirmed the quoted goaltender.md fragment is accurate, the fix
is complete across all three layers, and the underlying claim is sound
(goaltender.md's only "look away" language is scoped to dead-puck
stoppages, never a live puck, so the fix does not contradict any nuance in
the owning document). Two Minor findings, both fixed verbatim as
suggested: (1) a silently-lowered capital in the quoted fragment with no
`[x]` bracket disclosure — this document's own house convention elsewhere
(e.g. line 75) — fixed to `"[t]rack the puck..."`; (2) the Key focus
caveat lacked the ⚠️ marker its two sibling scope-narrowing bullets in the
same block both carry — added. Gates re-run clean after both fixes.
**Committed.**
