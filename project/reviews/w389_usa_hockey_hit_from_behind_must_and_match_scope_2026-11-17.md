## What changed

### USA Hockey's ejection for a hit from behind: "can" became "must"
The rule:
- 608(b) is a mandatory major plus game misconduct for a reckless or head-first hit.
- Casebook 608 Situation 1 says the major plus game misconduct, or a match, "must be called" for a forceful check at the boards, for a head-first hit, or for reckless endangerment.
- 620(b) is a mandatory major plus game misconduct for careless head or neck contact.

The corrections:
- **playing_without_the_puck KT10:** "ends your game in most books, and under USA Hockey whenever it is forceful at the boards, head first or reckless, or carelessly reaches their head or neck". This went 110 → 117 words; it was 120 before the safety reviewer's optional trim.
- **shooting KT5:** the same form. 103 → 121 words, by the author's whitespace count of the whole item.
- **defender KT2:** "Checking from behind ends it in most books, and under USA Hockey so does one that is forceful at the boards, head first or reckless, or that carelessly reaches their head or neck". 100 → 120 words, by the author's count.
  - The author's first fix used a semicolon before "so does careless contact". Read aloud, that sounded as if it applied in every book. Hockey Canada 7.6(a) makes accidental head contact a minor, so both reviewers flagged it. The final wording puts it under USA Hockey.
- **language_and_glossary KT12:** the safety reviewer's restructure. 122 → 148 words (whitespace count, list number and markup stripped).
  - The four cite groups that were read aloud are removed. The body and CM :507 keep them, which also settles the rules point that "(608)" alone did not cover the Casebook limb.
  - Every tier is kept.
  - It adds the mandatory Hockey Canada 7.5(c) / CARHA 53(b) match for driving a screener who cannot protect themselves into the net from behind. This omission predates the wave.
  - **CM :507** gains the same match with cites, so the KT and the CM agree.
- **shooting facts :287 (300/300).** "Driving into a goalie's back" said only "USA Hockey's floor is a minor and misconduct". The author's first fix wrote a closed list of three ejection triggers and left out 620(b); the safety reviewer rated this Major, because a careless hit to a goalie's mask is that case. Final wording: "USA Hockey: at least a minor and misconduct, ejecting if forceful at the boards, head first, reckless or careless to head or neck".
  - The author added "or neck" to the reviewer's text (620(b): "head, face or neck").
  - There was no room for a (608) cite. The rules-verifier ruled that acceptable, since no other book on that line carries a rule number either.

### passing_and_receiving: Hockey Canada minor/female head contact, scoped exactly
7.6(b)¶2's mandatory major plus game misconduct covers an infraction "that would otherwise call for a Minor penalty", which is the accidental case. ¶1 for violence is "may". ¶3 for a high cross-check is limited by Interpretation 2 to "a deliberate cross-checking action".
- **Body :474:** "A major and a game misconduct is at the referee's discretion, and mandatory if even accidental contact injures them, or for any deliberate cross-check above the shoulders, injury or not."
- **Facts :439 (298/300), rules-verifier text:** "…a third in a game ejects; major and game misconduct may come if violent, must for a deliberate high cross-check or if even accidental contact hurts; match if defenceless or meant to injure".
  - The author's first fix said "must for a high cross-check". The rules-verifier ruled it WRONG, in the harsher direction.
  - "(4.8(b))" was dropped, because 7.6(a)¶3 itself states the three-penalty ejection.
- **A gap in the book itself (rules-verifier):** no mandatory tier is written for intentional head contact that injures without intent to injure.
  - ¶2 is keyed to a minor. Intentional contact is a double minor, and Hockey Canada writes "would otherwise call for a double Minor" elsewhere when it means that.
  - "Even accidental" is a conservative reading, kept as safe.
