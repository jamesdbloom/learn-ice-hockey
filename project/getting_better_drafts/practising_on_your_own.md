# Practising on Your Own

> **Rule set:** Nothing here changes between rule sets. At a public session, the rink's posted rules decide what you can do.
>
> **Related:** [Finding More Ice](finding_more_ice.md) · [Practice and Development](practice_and_development.md) · [Skating](../technique/skating.md) · [Puck Handling](../technique/puck_handling.md) · [Conditioning and Recovery](conditioning_and_recovery.md)

## Key focus

**Spend public sessions on edges, turns and stops, not laps.** Laps feel like skating practice, but game skating is short bursts, sharp turns and stops.

**Work both feet and both directions, and give your weak side more reps.** Every one-foot drill here is done on the right foot and then the left.

**Once you have the shape, go hard.** A timid edge on a flat blade teaches nothing. Push as hard and as confidently as you can.

**Keep your head up, even when you reach down.** Looking at your skates drops your weight forward and flattens the blade.

**Do the edge work first, before the puck comes out.** At stick-and-puck, do your skating, then do the same moves with a puck.

**Read the posted rules, and use a clear patch of a quiet session.** Jumps, backward work and hard starts and stops need room. Don't sprint in a busy session.

**Off the ice, stickhandle with your head up, little and often.** Ten to fifteen focused minutes, often, beats a long session now and then.

## Overview

**Most players spend extra ice going round in circles.** Public skating is cheap ice, and it is best used on the edge work, turns and stops that a game asks for and that most players get little other time to practise. The drills below are a menu. Pick a few, do them on both feet, and do them hard once you have the shape.

**Stick-and-puck adds the puck.** Skate first, then repeat the same work with a puck. Off the ice, head-up stickhandling and wall passing are the work that transfers best; strength and mobility support your skating but are no substitute for it. [Practice and Development](practice_and_development.md) explains why some practice transfers and some doesn't. This page is the how.

## 1. Before you start

**In short:** The rink's rules come first. Find a quiet session and a clear patch of ice.

- **Check the posted rules.** At a public session, assume sticks and pucks are not allowed unless the rules say so.
- **Ask the desk which sessions are quietest.** A quiet session gives you room to work.
- **Keep jumps, backward skating, fast starts and hard stops to a clear patch** of a quiet session, and only if the posted rules allow them. A public session is full of children and new skaters. In a busy one, do the slow drills or just skate.
- **Look before you move.** Many of these drills travel sideways or backwards. Check over your shoulder first.
- **Wear a helmet for edge work, jumps and backward skating.** You will fall when you lean further than you have before. Learn how to fall first: [Falling and Getting Up](../technique/skating.md#falling-and-getting-up).
- **At stick-and-puck, ask what gear is required.** It is often only a helmet and gloves. Read the [drop-in and pick-up etiquette](../getting-started/getting_started.md#drop-in-and-pick-up-etiquette) before your first one: look before you cross the ice, don't skate through someone's drill, and collect your own pucks.

## 2. Using the session well

**In short:** Edges first, both feet, short and focused, then hard.

- **Edges first, while you are fresh.** [Skating](../technique/skating.md#where-to-spend-thirty-minutes-of-ice--a-coaching-order-not-a-measured-ranking) suggests an order for thirty minutes of ice: edge work, then weak-side crossovers, then stops and starts both ways, then transitions (forwards to backwards and back), then stride work, then the same again with a puck. That order is coaching judgement, so reorder it if a coach who has watched you says so.
- **Short and focused beats long and tired.** Ten to fifteen focused minutes of edge work, done often, is the guidance [Skating](../technique/skating.md#basic-edge-drills) follows. Edge work is not conditioning.
- **Slow to learn it, hard to own it.** Learn each drill slowly. Once it holds, do it as hard as you can. A drill that only ever feels comfortable is not making you better.
- **Wobbly is good; out of control is not.** Wobbling means you are at the edge of what you can do, which is where you improve. If you are out of control, slow down.
- **Pick two or three drills a session.** A few done well beat a dozen done once.
- **Get filmed.** You cannot see yourself skate. A friend with a phone, filming from the side and from behind, will show you faults you cannot feel.

**At stick-and-puck**, warm your edges up without a puck first, then add the puck to the same moves. Keep your head up when you carry it: the habit you practise is the one you will have in a game. [Puck Handling](../technique/puck_handling.md#bridge-it-to-the-ice) explains why off-ice hands need to be tried on skates.

## 3. Drills for a public session

**In short:** Pick a few from each group, do every one-foot drill on both feet, and go hard once you have the shape.

These drills are coaching craft, not a tested programme. Most of them fit most public sessions; section 1 says where to take the faster ones.

**Words used in the drills:**

- **Inside edge** — the edge of the blade on the big-toe side. **Outside edge** — the little-toe side. [Skating](../technique/skating.md#what-an-edge-is) explains edges.
- **Free foot** — the foot you are not skating on, held just off the ice.
- **C-cut** — pushing one skate out and curving it back under you, so the blade draws a C on the ice.
- **Crossover** — stepping one foot over in front of the other. The real push is the foot underneath, which is why coaches call it a **cross-under**.

### One-foot edges

#### Inside edge forward

**Trains:** grip and power on the inside edge, the edge every stride, crossover and tight turn pushes from.

1. Glide on your right foot, left foot lifted, knee well bent.
2. Push the right skate out to the side on its inside edge, hard, carving a C.
3. Curve it back under your body and go straight into the next C. Bend low enough to touch the ice with a hand. Keep going, as hard and as confidently as you can.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** small, timid Cs on a flat blade, so the skate skids instead of biting and you hardly move.

#### Outside edge forward: small step-overs

**Trains:** getting onto the outside edge quickly, as the inside foot does in every crossover.

1. Skate roughly straight down the ice with small, quick steps.
2. Step your left foot over in front of the right and land it on its outside edge.
3. Step your right foot over in front of the left and land it on its outside edge. Keep alternating.

**Common mistake:** landing the stepping foot flat or on its inside edge, so there is no curve and it is just a walk.

#### Outside edge forward: big lean-and-glide arcs

**Trains:** trusting a deep outside edge at speed, which lets you carve a wide turn without slowing.

1. Glide on your right foot and lean over onto its outside edge, carving a big arc to your right.
2. At the end of the arc, set your left foot down beside the right (don't cross) and lean onto its outside edge, carving a big arc to your left.
3. Keep switching feet, leaning further each time. Glide; don't step.

**Common mistake:** leaning only the upper body while the blade stays flat, so you tip over instead of curving.

#### Outside edge forward: big sharp C with a full step-over

**Trains:** a deep, sharp outside edge with a full crossover, the base of a tight, powerful turn.

1. Start facing the boards, on your right foot.
2. Carve a big, sharp C on the right outside edge, turning away from the boards and all the way round.
3. When you face the boards again, step the left foot right over in front, put the right foot back down and carve the next C.
4. Then do it on the left foot, going the other way.

**Common mistake:** opening the C into a wide, lazy curve because you don't lean far enough over the edge.

#### Inside edge backwards

**Trains:** power on the backward inside edge, the push a defenceman uses to skate backwards.

1. Skate backwards on your right foot, left foot lifted, knees well bent.
2. Push the heel out on the inside edge, hard, carving a C.
3. Pull it back under your body and go straight into the next C. Bend low enough to touch the ice with a hand.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** leaning forward over the toes, so the blade rocks off the edge and you stall.

#### Outside edge backwards, free foot in front

**Trains:** balance and power on the backward outside edge, which backward crossovers and pivots depend on.

1. Skate backwards on your right foot, left foot lifted and held in front of you.
2. Carve a C on the outside edge of the right skate, out to your left and curving back to your right.
3. Go straight into the next C, as hard as you can.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** looking down at the skates, which pitches the weight forward and flattens the blade.

#### Outside edge backwards, free foot behind

**Trains:** balance on the backward outside edge with the weight further back, as when you look over your shoulder skating backwards.

1. Skate backwards on your right foot, left foot lifted and held behind you, towards where you are going.
2. Carve a C on the outside edge of the right skate, out to your left and curving back to your right.
3. Go straight into the next C.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** sitting so far back on the heel that the blade slips out. Keep your weight over the middle of the blade.

#### One-foot slalom forwards

**Trains:** rolling from one edge to the other on one foot, the control behind tight weaving with the puck.

1. Push off one foot and lift it, so you are on the other foot only.
2. Roll onto the inside edge to curve round an imaginary point, or along a line on the ice.
3. Roll across to the outside edge to curve back the other way. Keep alternating to the end.
4. Do it on the right foot, then the left.

**Common mistake:** twisting the shoulders to steer while the blade stays flat, so the line wobbles instead of curving.

#### One-foot slalom backwards

**Trains:** edge-to-edge control skating backwards, which a defenceman uses to stay in front of an attacker who changes direction.

1. Skating backwards, push off one foot and lift it, so you are on the other foot only.
2. Roll onto the inside edge to curve one way.
3. Roll across to the outside edge to curve back the other way. Keep alternating to the end.
4. Do it on the right foot, then the left.

**Common mistake:** bending at the waist instead of the knees, so your weight is over the toes and the edges won't grip.

#### One-foot jump to the other foot

**Trains:** balance and leg drive from a deep crouch, landing on the other foot ready to skate, with your head up the whole time.

1. Glide forward on your right foot, knee bent, left foot lifted.
2. Crouch and touch the right skate with your hand, keeping your head up.
3. As you stand back up, jump onto your left foot.
4. Glide, crouch and touch the left skate, and jump back onto the right as you stand up. Keep it going.

**Common mistake:** looking down at the skate to touch it, so the head drops and your balance goes with it.

#### One-foot jump to the same foot

**Trains:** strength and balance on one leg, so you can recover on one foot after contact or a stumble.

1. Glide forward on your right foot, knee bent, left foot lifted.
2. Bend deeper and spring up and forward off the right foot.
3. Land back on the right foot, knee bent, without putting the left foot down.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** putting the free foot down as you land, which turns it into a two-foot landing.

#### One-foot crossover jump

**Trains:** crossover strength and balance, driving off one foot and landing the crossing foot cleanly on the far side, as in a quick change of direction.

1. Glide forward on your left foot, right foot lifted.
2. Jump your right foot over the left, crossing in front of it.
3. Land on the right foot on the far side, knee bent, as the left foot lifts.
4. Jump the left foot back over the right, and keep going both ways.

**Common mistake:** crossing the foot behind instead of in front, or landing on a straight leg.

#### Two-foot jump

**Trains:** leg power and a soft, balanced landing, the base for every other jump here.

1. Glide forward on both feet, shoulder width apart, knees bent.
2. Bend deeper and jump off both feet together.
3. Land on both feet at the same time, knees bent, and glide on.

**Common mistake:** landing stiff-legged or on the heels, which throws your weight back.

#### Two-foot 180 turn jump

**Trains:** turning quickly from forwards to backwards while keeping your balance, as a defenceman does to face the play.

1. Glide forward on both feet, knees bent.
2. Jump off both feet and turn half a circle in the air.
3. Land on both feet skating backwards, knees bent, still travelling the same way.
4. Next time, turn the other way.

**Common mistake:** turning only the shoulders, so the skates land part-way round and catch an edge.

#### Small figure of 8, inside edge

**Trains:** holding an inside edge all the way round a tight curve, the edge a tight turn and a crossover push from.

1. Start where the two circles meet and push onto your right foot.
2. Skate the whole right-hand circle on the right inside edge, curving to your left. Don't change edge.
3. Back in the middle, change feet and push onto your left foot.
4. Skate the whole left-hand circle on the left inside edge, curving to your right, back to the middle.

**Common mistake:** flattening the blade part-way round, so the circle opens up and doesn't come back to the middle.

#### Small figure of 8, outside edge

**Trains:** holding an outside edge round a curve, the edge the inside foot pushes from, under the body, in a crossover.

1. Start where the two circles meet and push onto your right foot.
2. Skate the whole right-hand circle on the right outside edge, curving to your right. Don't change edge.
3. Back in the middle, change feet and push onto your left foot.
4. Skate the whole left-hand circle on the left outside edge, curving to your left, back to the middle.

**Common mistake:** dropping onto the inside edge, or putting the free foot down, when the lean feels too far. Make the circles bigger at first if you need to.

### Mohawks

A **mohawk** here is the open-hip, heel-to-heel position: toes pointing opposite ways, heels in line. [Skating](../technique/skating.md#the-mohawk) explains it.

#### Mohawk side to side

**Trains:** opening the hips so you can move across the ice while still facing the play, the way a defenceman turns to follow an attacker.

1. Turn both feet out heel-to-heel: right toe pointing where you are going, left toe pointing back, heels in line, knees bent.
2. Glide across towards one side of your lane with your weight over the right foot.
3. At the side, turn your hips over and switch, so the left foot now points the way you go.
4. Glide back across, and keep switching all the way down the ice.

**Common mistake:** stiff, straight legs. Without bent knees the hips can't open and the heels drift apart.

#### Stepping mohawks side to side

**Trains:** quick, balanced hip turns with fast feet, so you can change direction while still facing the play.

1. Set up heel-to-heel as above, right foot leading.
2. Glide across towards one side of your lane.
3. At the side, take two quick steps: the left foot steps round to point the new way, then the right foot steps in behind it, heel to heel.
4. Glide back across with the left foot leading, and step again at the other side.

**Common mistake:** big, slow steps that open the feet wide apart, instead of quick, small steps that keep the heels close.

### Starts

Only on a clear patch, and never in a busy session.

#### Explosive V-start

**Trains:** getting to speed in the first few strides, which wins races to loose pucks.

1. Stand with heels close and toes turned out in a V, knees bent, leaning forward.
2. Run your first strides short and quick, toes out, pushing each foot out to the side.
3. Keep the next few strides short and fast, and stay low.
4. As you speed up, let the strides get longer and the toes come round to point forward.

**Common mistake:** standing up tall and taking long first strides, so you glide instead of driving.

#### Explosive crossover start

**Trains:** going from standing side-on to full speed sideways, as when a puck goes past you.

1. Stand side-on to where you want to go, knees bent.
2. Turn your lead foot to point where you are going, and turn your shoulders with it.
3. Cross the back foot over in front while the lead foot pushes hard under your body on its outside edge.
4. Drive away with short, fast strides. Repeat starting the other way.

**Common mistake:** standing up as you turn, so the push under your body goes into the air instead of the ice.

### Two-foot edges

#### Forward crossovers, two steps and stop

**Trains:** crossover confidence. Done very slowly, it teaches you to place the crossing foot and push under before you need to do it at speed.

1. Stand facing one way, knees bent. You will move sideways, not forwards.
2. Moving to your right, cross the left foot over in front of the right, then step the right foot out from behind. Do that twice.
3. Stop sideways on the inside edge of the right (leading) foot.
4. Go back to the left the same way, crossing the right foot over twice, and stop on the inside edge of the left foot. Keep going back and forth.

**Common mistake:** rushing it. The drill is worth most done slowly, with each crossing foot placed deliberately.

#### Backward crossovers, two or three steps and stop

**Trains:** moving backwards round a curve with speed and then stopping, the way a defenceman follows a rush and then holds their ground.

1. Skate backwards on a gentle curve and cross the outside foot over in front of the inside foot.
2. Do two or three crossovers, pushing under with the inside foot each time.
3. Push both heels out, toes turned in, knees bent, and stop on both feet.
4. Repeat curving the other way.

**Common mistake:** looking down at your feet, which tips you onto your toes and kills the crossovers.

#### Lemons forwards

**Trains:** inside-edge push and control on both feet at once, the base of every stride and stop.

1. Start with heels together and toes turned out, knees bent, astride a line.
2. Push both feet out on the inside edges.
3. Turn the toes in and bring the feet back together until the boots, ideally the blades, touch in the middle. Repeat.

**Common mistake:** stopping short of the middle, so the feet never come back together.

#### Lemons backwards

**Trains:** backward push and balance on both inside edges, the base of backward skating. It is harder than the forward version, and worth more.

1. Start with toes together and heels apart, knees bent, astride a line.
2. Push both heels out on the inside edges.
3. Turn the heels in and bring the feet back together until the boots, ideally the blades, touch in the middle. Repeat.

**Common mistake:** leaning forward over the toes, which stops the heels pushing out.

#### Crossing-under lemons forwards

**Trains:** rolling from inside edge to outside edge on both feet, and getting the feet right under the body, as a crossover push does.

1. Start with heels together and toes turned out, knees bent, astride a line.
2. Push both feet out on the inside edges.
3. As they come back in, roll the feet onto the outside edges and let them cross completely under your body.
4. Push out again from the crossed position. Do small lemons first, then wide ones.

**Common mistake:** stopping the feet at the middle on the inside edges, so they never roll over and never cross.

#### Crossing-under lemons backwards

**Trains:** rolling between inside and outside edges skating backwards, the feel behind backward crossovers.

1. Start with toes together and heels apart, knees bent, astride a line.
2. Push both heels out on the inside edges.
3. As they come back in, roll onto the outside edges and let the feet cross completely under your body.
4. Push out again from the crossed position. Small lemons first, then wide ones.

**Common mistake:** leaning forward over the toes, which stops the heels pushing out and the feet crossing.

### Turns

#### Stepping turn, forwards to backwards

**Trains:** turning from forwards to backwards without stopping, the way a defenceman turns to face the play.

1. Glide forwards on both feet, knees bent.
2. Turn your shoulders and hips the way you want to go, put your weight on one foot, and lift the other with its toe turned out.
3. Put that foot down pointing backwards, then lift the other foot and turn it round.
4. Put it down and glide backwards on both feet. Next time, turn the other way.

**Common mistake:** turning the feet before the shoulders and hips, so the step is wide and wobbly instead of heel close to heel.

#### Stepping 360, alternate directions

**Trains:** turning all the way round while still moving, so you can look behind you and keep your speed.

1. Glide forwards on both feet.
2. Turn to your right: step your right foot round to go backwards, then bring the left round.
3. Keep turning right: step the right foot round again to go forwards, then the left. That is a full circle.
4. Do the next full circle to your left, leading with the left foot each time.

**Common mistake:** stopping halfway round to reset, so it becomes two half turns instead of one smooth circle.

#### Three turn, forwards to backwards

**Trains:** turning round on one foot with balance, a quick turn from forwards to backwards.

1. From a slow glide, stand on your right foot on its outside edge, curving to your right.
2. Turn your shoulders and hips into the curve and rock onto the front of the blade, so the skate spins half a turn.
3. Come out gliding backwards on the inside edge of the same foot, still curving the same way.
4. Repeat on the left foot.

**Common mistake:** hopping, or putting the free foot down to get round, instead of letting the blade turn on the ice.

#### Forward anchor

**Trains:** a much tighter turn, using the inside foot as a pivot, so you can change direction in a small space.

1. Skate in forwards with a wide stance.
2. Plant the inside foot and anchor it on the heel of the skate: the heel stays in one spot while the toe swings round.
3. Carve the outside foot round the anchor on its inside edge.
4. Come out of the turn and skate away. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** letting the inside heel slide, so there is no anchor and the turn goes wide.

#### Forward soft drag

**Trains:** a tighter turn by letting the inside foot help it round, so you can change direction in a small space.

1. Skate in forwards, knees bent.
2. Put the inside foot's toe on the ice and let it drag on one spot while the heel swings round. It is the anchor turn on the toe instead of the heel.
3. Carve the outside foot round it on its inside edge.
4. Come out of the turn and skate away. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** pressing the toe down too hard, so it brakes and you stall in the turn.

#### Backward soft drag

**Trains:** turning sharply while skating backwards, as a defenceman does when the play changes direction.

1. Skate backwards, knees bent.
2. Put the inside foot's toe on the ice and let it drag on one spot to help the turn. Going backwards, only the toe works for this.
3. Carve the outside foot round it on its inside edge, in a sharp, tight C.
4. Come out of the turn and skate away backwards. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** looking down at the feet, so the shoulders drop and the turn wobbles.

#### Continuous crossovers forwards

**Trains:** building speed through turns and changing direction without stopping, which is how you skate in the flow of a game.

1. Skating forwards, cross the outside foot over in front.
2. Think cross-under: the real push is the inside foot driving out under your body on its outside edge.
3. Step onto the inside edge of that foot, which starts the curve the other way.
4. Cross over the other way and push under again. Keep going without stopping.

**Common mistake:** stepping the outside foot over without the push underneath, so the crossover just walks round the curve and adds no speed.

#### Continuous crossovers backwards

**Trains:** backward power and changes of direction without stopping, the way a defenceman stays in front of a rush that cuts across.

1. Skating backwards, cross the outside foot over in front, on the side you are facing.
2. Think cross-under: the real push is the inside foot driving out under your body on its outside edge.
3. Step onto the inside edge of that foot, which starts the curve the other way.
4. Cross over the other way and push under again. Keep going without stopping.

**Common mistake:** stepping the outside foot over without the push underneath, so you add no speed.

#### Tight turns

**Trains:** turning sharply without losing speed, so you can change direction to follow the puck or your check.

1. Skate in on two feet, knees bent, and open into a scissor stance: inside leg well forward, outside leg back.
2. Turn round a point holding the scissor: lean both blades in, inside foot on its outside edge, outside foot on its inside edge, head pointing where you are going.
3. Stay low as you come round, then stride away the way you came. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** standing up in the turn, so the edges let go and the turn goes wide.

#### Jam turns

**Trains:** changing direction in the shortest space, for when the play reverses and you have to go back the other way now.

1. Skate in with some speed.
2. Sink and swing both skates hard into the turn so the blades bite and spray snow, nearly stopping, still on two feet.
3. Push hard out of the turn and stride away the way you came. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** leaning back as the skates bite, so you stall or fall instead of driving out.

#### Punch jams

**Trains:** turning back on the play and getting to full speed in the new direction in one or two steps.

1. Skate in with some speed.
2. Jam the turn: both skates swing round and bite, spraying snow.
3. Without pausing, punch out: the outside foot crosses over in front and the inside foot pushes hard underneath.
4. Keep driving with short, quick strides. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** stopping dead after the jam and then starting again, instead of turning straight into the crossover.

#### Punch turns

**Trains:** coming out of a turn faster than you went in, which wins races to loose pucks.

1. Skate in on two feet, knees bent.
2. Make a tight turn round a point, inside foot leading, both blades leaning in. The blades carve; they don't skid.
3. As you come out, punch: the outside foot crosses over in front and the inside foot pushes hard underneath.
4. Keep accelerating with short, quick strides. Repeat turning the other way.

**Common mistake:** standing up as you leave the turn, so the crossover pushes you sideways instead of forward.

#### Small-circle pivots

**Trains:** switching between forwards and backwards while always facing the play, which defencemen and backcheckers do every shift.

1. Face one way, say one end of the rink, and keep facing it all the way round a small circle. Skate forwards round the first side.
2. Pivot from forwards to backwards on the front of both skates, heels swinging round, ideally with a small hop so the heels don't cut the ice.
3. Skate backwards round the far side, still facing the same way.
4. Step from backwards to forwards: with the outside leg travelling backwards, step straight onto the other leg going forwards, without stopping. Then go round the other way.

**Common mistake:** turning the chest with the feet, so you end up facing round the circle instead of facing one way.

### Stops

Hard stops go on a clear patch. [Skating](../technique/skating.md#stops-and-starts) covers the technique.

#### One-foot stop in front

**Trains:** a controlled first stop, and the feel of scraping ice with an inside edge, which every harder stop builds on.

1. Glide forward on both feet, knees bent, feet about hip-width apart.
2. Turn your right toe in and heel out, and slide that foot a little in front of you.
3. Press onto the inside edge so it scrapes the ice, keep the left foot gliding straight, and sink your knees until you stop.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** leaning forward over the toes, so the scraping foot catches and tips you instead of slowing you.

#### Outside-edge T-stop

**Trains:** slowing down while staying square to the play, and control of the outside edge of the back foot.

1. Glide forward on your right foot, left foot lifted.
2. Bring the left foot behind your right heel at a right angle, making a T, toe pointing out.
3. Lower it and drag it on its outside edge, pressing a little harder as you go. Keep your shoulders and hips facing forward and your knees bent until you stop.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** twisting the hips round towards the back foot, which turns you sideways instead of stopping you.

#### Two-foot hockey stop, parallel

**Trains:** the quick, full stop players use to change direction, stay with a check, or arrive at the puck under control.

1. Glide forward on both feet, knees bent.
2. Turn your hips and both blades together across the line of travel, keeping the blades parallel.
3. Sink your knees and lean back against the slide, front foot on its inside edge, back foot on its outside edge, until you stop.
4. Practise turning both ways.

**Common mistake:** turning only the shoulders or only one foot, so the blades end up at different angles and you skid round instead of stopping.

#### Two-foot hockey stop, side by side

**Trains:** a stable, strong stop with the feet split front to back, which keeps you balanced when stopping hard.

1. Glide forward on both feet, knees bent.
2. Turn both blades across the line of travel together, and split the feet into a scissor stance: inside skate well ahead of the outside skate.
3. Sink your knees and lean back against the slide, front foot on its inside edge, back foot on its outside edge, until you stop.
4. Practise turning both ways.

**Common mistake:** letting the feet come level, which turns it back into the parallel stop.

#### One-foot glide to one-foot stop, forwards, inside edge

**Trains:** balance and edge control to stop on one foot, so you can stop and push off again in one movement.

1. Glide forward on your right foot, left foot lifted beside it.
2. Turn your hips and the right blade across the line of travel, toe turning towards the lifted foot.
3. Sink the knee and press the inside edge until you stop, keeping the left foot off the ice.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** putting the lifted foot down to save your balance, which turns it back into a two-foot stop.

#### One-foot glide to one-foot stop, forwards, outside edge

**Trains:** stopping on the outside edge of one foot, the edge you need to stop and go the other way in one movement.

1. Glide forward on your right foot, left foot lifted.
2. Turn the right toe out to the right, so the right heel comes round in front of the left foot and the blade is across the line of travel.
3. Sink the knee and press the outside edge until you stop, keeping the left foot off the ice.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** leaning the upper body forward over the stopping foot, so the outside edge slips out.

#### One-foot glide to one-foot stop, backwards

**Trains:** stopping when skating backwards, which anyone covering an attacker needs before they can push forward again.

1. Skate backwards and glide on your right foot, left foot lifted.
2. Turn the right toe out and the heel in.
3. Bend the knee and press the blade outwards on its inside edge so it scrapes the ice until you stop.
4. Repeat on the other foot.

**Common mistake:** leaning back over the heels, so the blade slides out from under you.

## 4. Off-ice stick work

**In short:** Head up, a smooth surface, ten to fifteen minutes, often.

Stickhandling is the one hockey skill you can really improve without ice, and [Practice and Development](practice_and_development.md#off-ice-practice-that-genuinely-transfers) rates it, with wall passing, as the off-ice work that transfers best.

- **Keep your head up on every rep.** Watch the television or call out numbers on a wall. Head-down reps at home become the habit that shows up in games.
- **Get a smooth surface first** — a shooting pad or plastic tiles. Without one a puck won't slide.
- **Little and often.** Ten to fifteen focused minutes, a few times a week, beats a long session once. Leave the pad and a ball where you walk past them.
- **Pass against a wall or a rebounder, half on the backhand.** It gives you hundreds of receptions in ten minutes.
- **Shoot off a pad, not concrete,** and vary where you shoot from.
- **Check the space yourself.** Clearance from people, windows and glass, safe footing, and supervision for a child are for whoever is there to judge. This page cannot judge your room or driveway.

[Puck Handling](../technique/puck_handling.md#how-to-practise) has the kit, a starter set of drills and a ladder for making each one harder.

## 5. Off-ice exercises

**In short:** Follow [Conditioning and Recovery](conditioning_and_recovery.md). This page adds no programme.

Off-ice training doesn't teach skating, but it removes the limits that stop you doing it: single-leg strength, ankle stability and hip mobility. [Skating](../technique/skating.md#off-ice-work) names the most useful work, and [Conditioning and Recovery](conditioning_and_recovery.md#training-for-the-adult-recreational-player) sets out a realistic week, how much to do and how to build up.

- **Warm up first.** [Conditioning and Recovery](conditioning_and_recovery.md#the-off-ice-warm-up-most-amateurs-skip-entirely) has a ten-to-fifteen-minute warm-up.
- **Get coached on the lifts** before you add weight.
- **Build up gradually.** A sudden jump in training is a common way for amateurs to get hurt.
- **Young players:** see [Youth Players](conditioning_and_recovery.md#youth-players). Get qualified coaching before a young player lifts at all.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skating laps at a public session.** Spend the time on edges, turns and stops.
- **Only practising your good side.** Do every one-foot drill on both feet and every turn both ways, and give the weak side more.
- **Timid edges.** A flat blade teaches nothing. Once you have the shape, push hard.
- **Looking at your skates.** Your weight goes forward and the blade flattens. Keep your head up.
- **Sprinting, jumping or skating backwards in a busy session.** Use a clear patch of a quiet one, if the posted rules allow it.
- **Taking a stick to a public session without checking the rules.**
- **Trying a dozen drills once.** Pick two or three and do them well.
- **Stickhandling at home with your head down.** It builds the habit you'll have in games.
- **Going from nothing to a full off-ice programme in a week.** Build up gradually, as [Conditioning and Recovery](conditioning_and_recovery.md) explains.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Use public sessions for edges, turns and stops,** not laps.
2. **Edges first, while you are fresh,** then the rest, then the same with a puck at stick-and-puck.
3. **Do every one-foot drill on both feet, and every turn both ways.** Give the weak side more.
4. **Learn it slowly, then do it hard.** A timid edge on a flat blade teaches nothing.
5. **Keep your head up,** even when you reach down to touch the ice or your skate.
6. **Read the posted rules and find a clear patch of a quiet session** for jumps, backward work and hard starts and stops.
7. **Off the ice, stickhandle with your head up, little and often,** and follow [Conditioning and Recovery](conditioning_and_recovery.md) for strength work.
