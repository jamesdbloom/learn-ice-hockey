# Problems found

## 1. The format is specified at `##` level but only works at `###` level

`## Defensive Zone`, `## Neutral Zone` and `## Offensive Zone` are one- or two-sentence containers
(lines 30, 135, 206). Each yields exactly **2** facts, below the 3-fact minimum, and those facts
are a restatement of the heading. `## Other Situations` (272) yields **0**. The worked example
supplied as the quality bar is itself drawn from a `###` subsection, which confirms the mismatch.

**Recommendation:** put blocks on `###` subsections and on the two flat `##` sections that have
real prose (`Overview`, `A Note on Language`). Skip the four container headings, or accept 2-fact
blocks there.

*(Related: the example block mixes sources across two subsections — "Position / Action / Targets /
Never" are from `Carrying the Puck — Free Space` (36–54) but "Risk: Slot turnovers lead to direct
scoring chances" is from `Carrying the Puck — Under Pressure` (66). If facts are to be strictly
traceable to the section they sit under, the example is not itself conformant.)*

## 2. The 3–6 cap forces dropping supported material, including rules

Five sections carry substantially more than six extractable facts, and in each the cap forces a
choice between rulebook material and technique material:

| Section | Extractable | Casualties of note |
|---|---|---|
| NZ `Pressuring an Opponent with the Puck` (163–188) | ~12 | boards-side pivot is a **coaching default, not a law** (176); the step-up vs pinch distinction the style guide singles out (185–188) |
| DZ `Carrying the Puck — Under Pressure` (58–80) | ~12 | **rim vs reverse — "a core defenceman skill"** (68); never take trapezoid dimensions from Rule 27.8 (74) |
| OZ `Supporting the Cycle from the Line` (246–266) | ~10 | activate on possession not hope (262); call it (263); late not early (264) |
| `Blocking Shots` (310–321) | ~10 | **on a 2-on-1, don't block — your goalie wants the shot** (319) |
| DZ `Weak-Side Coverage at the Goalmouth` (107–129) | ~9 | "position beats strength is a tendency, not a law" (120); the high-slot rotation and its exception (123, 125) |

Every one of these sections is long because the body itself flags the dropped material as
consequential. A reader who reads only the fact block gets a materially thinner and, in the
rim/reverse and 2-on-1-blocking cases, a *differently weighted* document. **Either raise the cap to
8 for these five sections, or split the long `###` sections.**

## 3. `Common Mistakes` and `Key Takeaways` gain nothing

Both sections are already lists of terse standalone one-liners — the format's own output shape. A
fact block above them is a shortened restatement of the twelve/eight bullets immediately below it.
Worse, in `Common Mistakes` every single fact takes the `Never:` label, so the label column carries
zero information and the block is visually inert. **Recommend no blocks on these two sections.**

There is also a corpus-hygiene risk here that the style guide names directly (line 263: "the
summary layer is where corrections go to die"). Adding a third summary layer above two existing
summary layers creates one more place for a correction to fail to reach.

## 4. The approved label set has no label for "coaching choice"

This is the sharpest conflict with the cardinal rule. The document hedges at least seven claims
explicitly, and the only available label for them is `Key:`, which also carries plain definitional
facts. The hedged claims:

| Claim | Body's own hedge | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Only one defenceman pinches at a time | "near-universal coaching convention rather than a rule" | 242, 256, 365 |
| Pivot to the boards side | "a coaching default, not a law" | 176 |
| Position beats strength | "a tendency, not a law" | 120 |
| 2-on-1: take the pass | "a convention, not a law", with a stated caveat | 335, 361 |
| 3-on-2 shape | "two named systems… a coaching choice, not a law" | 336 |
| Winger help in the corner | "a coaching choice, not a law of hockey" | 96 |
| Breakout options list | "not as a ranking of breakout plays" | 42 |
| How much your team blocks shots | "a coaching choice" | 321 |
| Weak-side activation | "the common default, not the only route" | 261 |
| Both-D-to-the-puck as a cause of goals | "coaching consensus, not a statistic" | 344 |

Keeping these honest costs 12–20 extra words each, which fights the "terse imperative" brief
directly — several of my `Key:` lines run to two clause-lengths for exactly this reason.

**Recommendation: add a `Convention:` or `System:` label to the approved set.** It would let the
hedge live in the label rather than the text, keep the lines terse, and — more importantly — make
the cardinal-rule distinction visible at a glance, which is precisely what this corpus keeps
failing at.
